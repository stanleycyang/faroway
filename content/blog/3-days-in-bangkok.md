# 3 Days in Bangkok: The Perfect City Itinerary

Bangkok operates at a frequency most cities don't know exists. It's loud, fragrant (in all directions), perpetually overheated, deeply Buddhist, and home to some of the best food on the planet — most of it served from carts at the side of the road for $1. Three days is enough to understand why people keep coming back.

## Day 1: Old Bangkok — Temples, Canals, and the Grand Palace

### Morning (7:30 AM – 1:00 PM)

Bangkok is best beaten at dawn before the heat. Start at **Wat Pho** (Temple of the Reclining Buddha) when it opens at 8 AM — the 46-metre-long gold Buddha fills its entire hall, head resting on a pillow, feet inlaid with 108 mother-of-pearl panels depicting auspicious characteristics. The temple complex surrounding it contains 100+ smaller chedis, courtyards of seated Buddhas, and the original Thai massage school (book a 60-minute traditional massage for ฿260 — it's the birthplace of the discipline). Entry: ฿200. Budget 90 minutes.

Walk 10 minutes north to the **Grand Palace** complex (฿500 entry, includes Wat Phra Kaew). The Emerald Buddha inside Wat Phra Kaew is Thailand's most sacred religious object — only 66cm tall, carved from a single block of green jasper (not emerald), dressed in different golden robes by the king for each season. The surrounding temple complex, with its glittering mosaic-covered chedis and gilded yaksha (demon guardian) statues, is overwhelmingly, jaw-droppingly ornate. Budget 2 hours minimum. Dress code enforced: shoulders and knees covered.

**Morning fuel:** Across the river at **Tha Tien Market** — vendors sell *pad kra pao* (holy basil stir-fry) and *khao man gai* (steamed chicken over rice) from early morning for ฿60–80 per plate. This is where Bangkok temple workers eat. Join them.

### Afternoon (1:00 PM – 6:00 PM)

Take a **long-tail boat** from Tha Tien Pier (฿500–800 for a private hour tour, or jump on the shared Chao Phraya Express for ฿15 per stop) through the klongs — Bangkok's canal network that predates the road system by centuries. The canal water is brown and the pace of life along the banks is nothing like the city above: wooden houses on stilts, kids jumping in, monks in orange robes on temple docks. Take the express boat to Khlong San for:

**Wat Arun** (Temple of Dawn) — arguably Bangkok's most beautiful temple, its central 70-metre prang (Khmer-style tower) completely encrusted with Chinese porcelain fragments that glitter in afternoon light. Climb the steep stairs to halfway up for views over the river. Entry: ฿100.

Spend late afternoon in the **Rattanakosin District** walking the streets between temples: old shophouses, monks collecting alms, street vendors selling *mango sticky rice* (฿60, accept no substitutes).

### Evening (6:00 PM – 11:00 PM)

**Chinatown (Yaowarat)** for dinner — Bangkok's Chinatown is the best in Southeast Asia for street food, particularly after dark when the cooking carts take over Yaowarat Road. The classics: *goong tod* (fried shrimp), *hoy thod* (crispy oyster omelette), BBQ pork on rice, and for the brave, *kway chap* (offal soup with flat rice noodles that tastes far better than it sounds). Budget ฿200–300 for a full spread from multiple carts.

Drinks at **Teens of Thailand** — a gin bar in a converted shophouse on Soi Nana in Chinatown that opened Bangkok's natural wine and craft cocktail scene. Small, intimate, and the cocktails use ingredients from the fruit carts outside the door.

## Day 2: Modern Bangkok — Markets, Malls, and Rooftops

### Morning (9:00 AM – 1:00 PM)

Take the BTS Skytrain to Mo Chit and walk to **Chatuchak Weekend Market** — it only runs Saturday and Sunday, 35 acres, 15,000 stalls, and the most concentrated shopping experience on Earth. Navigate by the numbered sections: Section 2–4 for antiques and collectibles, 6–8 for furniture and ceramics, 26–28 for clothing, 27 specifically for vintage and Thai street fashion. Budget 2–3 hours and ฿1,000–3,000 depending on willpower. Get a fresh coconut (฿50) every 30 minutes to stay hydrated.

### Afternoon (1:00 PM – 7:00 PM)

Lunch at **Or Tor Kor Market** directly across from Chatuchak — a covered wet/dry market considered the highest quality fresh market in Bangkok. The prepared food section has some of the best *khao niaow ma muang* (mango sticky rice) in the city, plus *som tam* (papaya salad) made fresh to your spice specification. ฿80–120 per dish.

Spend mid-afternoon at **Talad Rod Fai** (Train Night Market) in the afternoon for browsing, or take the BTS to **Asok** and explore the **Terminal 21** shopping mall — seven floors where each floor is a different city (Tokyo, London, San Francisco, Hollywood) with corresponding shops and restaurants. The food court on basement level is one of Bangkok's best-value lunch spots (฿60–100 per dish, unlike every other mall in Bangkok).

Late afternoon: walk the canal path along **Saen Saep** in the Pratunam neighbourhood — the sky is lower here, the streets older, and the floating market vendors are still out at 5 PM.

### Evening (7:00 PM – midnight)

**Sky bar at Lebua State Tower** — the "Hangover 2" bar at 820 feet above Bangkok, arguably the most cinematic rooftop in Southeast Asia. Cocktails are ฿600–900 (smart casual dress enforced). Go at 7 PM for sunset over the city, stay for two drinks, then descend for a real dinner at street level.

For dinner: **Raan Jay Fai** on Mahachai Road — run by 70-something Michelin-starred chef Jay Fai who still cooks every dish herself wearing ski goggles against the charcoal heat. Her *crab omelette* (฿1,000) and *drunken noodles* (฿350) are worth the 2-hour queue. Arrive before 5 PM or after 8 PM to minimize the wait.

## Day 3: Off the Tourist Trail — Local Bangkok

### Morning (8:00 AM – 12:00 PM)

The Bangkok nobody photographs: take the MRT to **Hua Lamphong** station and walk north through **Pahurat** — Bangkok's Indian quarter, where fabric merchants sell silk and embroidered cloth from covered market halls and the smell of biryani and incense is overwhelming. Walk east into **Sampeng Lane** (Soi Wanit 1) — a covered market street one metre wide and 1.5 kilometres long, selling wholesale goods: plastic containers, hair accessories, cheap electronics, and fabric at vendor prices.

Breakfast at **Panthip Plaza** café (if you're in the tech district) or better: stop at any BTS station exit and find the cart selling *pa thong ko* (Chinese fried dough) with *nam tao hoo* (warm soy milk) — ฿35 total. The most Bangkok breakfast possible.

Continue north to **Dusit district**: **Vimanmek Mansion** is the world's largest golden teak building, built in 1900 as a royal residence. The interior is full of Victorian-era royal artefacts and the garden is peaceful. Entry ฿100 (included with Grand Palace ticket if you kept the stub). Adjacent **Ananta Samakhom Throne Hall** is a European-style Baroque dome that looks more Vienna than Bangkok and is astonishing for exactly that reason.

### Afternoon (12:00 PM – 6:00 PM)

Lunch in the **Ari neighbourhood** — Bangkok's most liveable area, where office workers and young Thais eat: Japanese curry shops, excellent *khao tom* (rice porridge) restaurants, and a branch of **Aroi** for the city's best *green curry* (฿120).

Spend the afternoon at **Jim Thompson House** — the former home of the American businessman who revived Thailand's silk industry and mysteriously disappeared in Malaysian jungle in 1967. The house is a collection of six 17th and 18th-century Thai buildings transplanted and joined together, full of Asian art and antiques. The guided tour is genuinely fascinating. Entry ฿200.

Final evening: **Khlong Toei Market** in the afternoon is Bangkok's largest wet market and one of its most intense sensory experiences — fish, meat, and produce stalls under a highway overpass where Thai chefs and home cooks have been shopping for decades. Not for the squeamish, completely unmissable.

### Evening (sunset onwards)

Drinks at **Octave Rooftop** at the Bangkok Marriott Sukhumvit (three levels, rotating bar on top, 360-degree views, cocktails ฿450) — less famous than Lebua but better views of the city grid.

Farewell dinner at **Boon Tong Kee** near Silom — Singaporean-style Hainanese chicken rice that somehow migrated to Bangkok and improved in transit. A whole chicken serves two for ฿350. Come at 7:30 PM when the dinner rush hits and it feels like the most alive restaurant in the city.

## Getting There & Around

Fly into Suvarnabhumi (BKK) or Don Mueang (DMK) — both well-connected by rail into the city. Suvarnabhumi has the Airport Rail Link (฿45, 30 minutes to Makkasan). Within Bangkok: BTS Skytrain and MRT cover all major areas; get a **Rabbit Card** for tap-and-go. Taxis start at ฿35 and most rides within the city run ฿80–150 — always insist on the meter.
