# 3 Days in Bali: The Perfect Island Itinerary

Bali has been over-discovered and over-photographed and still somehow manages to live up to its reputation. The rice terraces are as green as the pictures. The temples are genuinely spiritual. The food is extraordinary and cheap. Three days splits neatly between Ubud in the hills and the south coast — here's how to do both.

## Quick Facts

## Day 1: Ubud — Rice Terraces, Temples, and the Monkey Forest

### Morning (7:00 AM – 12:00 PM)

Get to the **Tegallalang Rice Terraces** by 7 AM before the heat and the photo tourists arrive. The cascading terraces north of Ubud are the image most associated with Bali — three tiers of bright green stepped fields maintained by a traditional irrigation system called *subak* (UNESCO-listed). Walk the full lower loop (45 minutes), stop at the swing platforms if you're into that sort of thing (₹35,000, roughly $2.30), and be back in Ubud by 9:30 AM.

Head to **Pura Tirta Empul** — a Hindu water temple where Balinese devotees perform a purification ritual in sacred spring pools. You can join the *melukat* bathing ritual (rent a sarong at the entrance for ₹30,000) or simply watch from the surrounding grounds. The ritual itself — entering the pool in order, submerging beneath each of the carved water spouts — is one of the most moving religious experiences you can participate in as a visitor anywhere in Southeast Asia. Arrive by 9 AM for the most activity.

**Morning fuel:** Breakfast at **Kafe** on Jalan Hanoman in Ubud — excellent smoothie bowls, avocado toast, and Balinese coffee that's nothing like the *kopi luwak* tourist-trap version (though that exists everywhere here too). Budget: ₹60,000–80,000 ($4–5).

### Afternoon (12:00 PM – 6:00 PM)

Lunch at a **warung** — a small family-run restaurant — on the side streets off Ubud's main market. The standard order: *nasi campur* (rice with small portions of 5-6 dishes arranged around it) for ₹25,000–35,000 ($1.50–2.25). It's the meal Balinese people actually eat and it's better than most restaurant main courses anywhere.

Spend the afternoon at the **Ubud Monkey Forest** (₹50,000/$3.30) — 700 Balinese long-tailed macaques living in a forest containing three 14th-century temples. The monkeys are wild, bold, and will steal your glasses if you wear them as a headband (don't). Allow 90 minutes. Then walk the main road of Ubud for craft shopping: silver jewellery, woodcarvings, batik fabric, and handmade paper at fixed-price shops (better deals than the market).

Late afternoon: arrange a **traditional Balinese massage** at one of the dozen spas on Jalan Bisma or Jalan Sugriwa — full-body 90-minute treatment for ₹130,000–200,000 ($8.50–13). Balinese massage is different from Thai — gentler strokes, coconut oil, less aggressive pressure. The perfect end to a day of walking.

### Evening (6:00 PM – 10:00 PM)

Watch the **Kecak Fire Dance** at Pura Uluwatu or at Ubud's Arma Museum (performances at 7:30 PM most evenings, ₹100,000/$6.50). The Kecak is performed by 50-100 men in a circle, creating a rhythmic chanting chorus that accompanies a Ramayana episode. The fire at the end is genuinely dramatic. Book tickets at your hotel or at the gate.

Dinner at **Locavore** if you've booked well in advance — it consistently ranks as one of Southeast Asia's best restaurants (₹1,100,000/$72 for the tasting menu, worth every cent). For a more accessible option, **Warung Sopa** on Jalan Sugriwa does excellent Balinese home cooking for ₹60,000–80,000 per person.

## Day 2: Between Worlds — Waterfalls, Volcanic Views, and Holy Springs

### Morning (7:00 AM – 1:00 PM)

Hire a driver for the day ($35–45) — Day 2 requires wheels. Start at **Tirta Gangga** water palace in east Bali (1 hour from Ubud), a royal bathing complex of ponds and fountains arranged in tiered gardens with Mount Agung as the backdrop. Walk the stone path over the stepping stones in the main pool and have coffee at the small café on-site. Entry: ₹20,000.

From Tirta Gangga, drive 30 minutes to **Lempuyang Temple** — one of Bali's oldest and most sacred temples perched on the slopes of Mount Lempuyang. The famous "Gates of Heaven" photograph (the split gates framing Mount Agung's reflection in a pool) requires a tripod, a patient photographer, and a wait in queue of about 20 minutes. The temple complex itself, if you're willing to climb 1,700 stone steps to the summit, rewards with views over east Bali that make the climb completely worth it. Budget 2 hours total.

### Afternoon (1:00 PM – 6:00 PM)

Lunch roadside at a warung near Sidemen — the small valley village southeast of Ubud has the quietest and most beautiful rice terrace landscape in Bali, without the crowds of Tegallalang. Several warungs serve lunch on terraced platforms with views over the fields. ₹40,000–60,000.

Drive to **Kehen Temple** in Bangli on the return — an 11th-century temple set on a hillside with massive banyan trees surrounding it and stunning carved stone gates leading to an inner courtyard with a three-tiered meru (pagoda). One of the most architecturally significant temples on the island and almost nobody visits it. Free.

Stop at **Gunung Kawi** on the way back to Ubud — a 10th-century royal monument of rock-cut shrines carved into a cliff face above the Pakerisan River. 315 steps down. 315 steps back up. Worth it. Entry: ₹50,000.

### Evening (6:00 PM – 10:00 PM)

Back in Ubud for dinner — **Naughty Nuri's** on Jalan Raya Sanggingan for pork ribs that are deeply un-touristy in a Hindu island context (Balinese Hinduism permits pork), incredibly good, and ₹120,000 for a full rack. The warung is cash-only, permanently crowded, and the free-poured martinis are famous locally.

Walk the **Campuhan Ridge** after dinner if there's a moon — a narrow ridge path between two river valleys north of the Tjampuhan Hotel bridge. No artificial lighting, just the outline of the hills against a dark sky.

## Day 3: South Bali — Temples, Beach Clubs, and Sunset

### Morning (8:00 AM – 12:00 PM)

Drive south to the **Uluwatu Peninsula** (1.5 hours). **Pura Luhur Uluwatu** sits on a 70-metre cliff above the Indian Ocean — one of Bali's six directional temples, it commands views up and down the Bukit coastline. Arrive at 8 AM before the heat and the groups. Wear a sarong (provided at entrance, ₹20,000 donation). Watch for the same macaques from Ubud — they're here too, and just as bold.

Below Uluwatu, **Padang Padang Beach** is a 15-minute walk down through a cave in the cliff face — a tiny pocket beach of white sand and turquoise water that starred in *Eat Pray Love* and deserves the reputation. Arrive before 10 AM for a beach chair without fighting. Entry: ₹10,000.

### Afternoon (12:00 PM – Sunset)

Lunch at **Single Fin** — a surf café above Uluwatu's main left-hand break, with views directly over the wave. Grilled fish and cold beer while watching surfers drop into overhead sets. ₹80,000–120,000 for a meal.

Spend mid-afternoon at **Finns Beach Club** in Canggu or **Potato Head** in Seminyak — two of Bali's most famous beach clubs. Day access with a food/drink minimum (₹200,000–400,000 depending on day/season). Infinity pool, DJ, ocean-facing sun loungers, cocktails at $8. This is where half of Bali's Instagram content originates.

**Sunset from Tanah Lot Temple**: Drive 30 minutes north from Seminyak to this sea temple built on a rock formation 100 metres offshore. At low tide you can wade out to touch the temple base; at high tide it's completely surrounded by ocean. The sun sets directly behind it. Every guidebook in the world recommends this for a reason — it's spectacular. Get there 45 minutes before sunset (₹60,000 entry).

### Evening

Dinner in **Seminyak** — the tourist strip has every type of restaurant at every price point but the best value is still local: **Warung Taman Bambu** near the beach for enormous portions of Indonesian grilled fish at ₹80,000–100,000, or **La Lucciola** right on the beach for an Italian-Balinese fusion blowout at ₹250,000–350,000 per person.

## Getting There & Around

Fly into Ngurah Rai International Airport (DPS), 20 minutes south of Seminyak. Take a fixed-rate airport taxi (₹250,000 to Seminyak, ₹350,000 to Ubud) — ignore the touts and book at the official counter. Use **Grab** for all shorter rides within south Bali; it's 30–50% cheaper than taxis. For a day trip to Ubud from the south, hiring a driver for the day ($40–50) is the most efficient option.
