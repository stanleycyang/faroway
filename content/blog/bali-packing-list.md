# Bali Packing List: What to Pack for Your Trip

Bali is tropical, humid, religiously Hindu, and culturally conservative outside the beach resort areas — which means your packing list needs to account for 30°C heat, sudden rain showers, temple dress codes, and the fact that you'll be removing your shoes 10 times a day. Here's what actually works.

---

## The Essentials

| Item | Why You Need It | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Sarong** | Temple entry requirement | Buy locally for $3-5 or bring a lightweight scarf |
| **Reef-safe sunscreen** | Coral protection + strong UV | Bali's sun at 8° south latitude is brutal |
| **Quick-dry towel** | Beach/pool/waterfall swimming | Hotels provide towels but they take forever to dry |
| **Water shoes** | Rocky beaches, temple streams | Bali's beaches aren't all soft sand |
| **Anti-chafe balm** | Humidity + walking = friction | BodyGlide or similar |

---

## Clothing (Climate + Culture)

Bali sits 8° south of the equator. It's hot (28-32°C year-round) and humid (70-90%). You'll sweat through clothes quickly. Pack light, breathable, and plan to do laundry.

### For Men
- [ ] 4-5 **lightweight t-shirts** (cotton or moisture-wicking blend)
- [ ] 2 **short-sleeve button-ups** (for nicer dinners/temple visits)
- [ ] 2-3 **shorts** (quick-dry fabric, at or below knee for temples)
- [ ] 1 pair **lightweight long pants** (temples require covered legs; linen works)
- [ ] **Swimwear** (2 pairs — one dries while you wear the other)
- [ ] Light rain jacket or packable poncho (afternoon storms are common)
- [ ] 1 **long-sleeve shirt** (sun protection, air-conditioned restaurants, mosques)

### For Women
- [ ] 3-4 **lightweight tops** (tank tops for the beach, modest t-shirts for temples)
- [ ] 1-2 **maxi dresses or skirts** (temple-appropriate + easy in the heat)
- [ ] **Shorts** (knee-length minimum for temple areas)
- [ ] **Swimwear** (bikinis fine at beach clubs, one-piece for local beaches)
- [ ] **Sarong or large scarf** (temple wrap, beach cover-up, emergency modesty layer)
- [ ] Light **cardigan** (over-air-conditioned malls and restaurants)
- [ ] 1 pair **lightweight pants** (for cooler evenings in Ubud)

### Temple Dress Code (Enforced)
Temples require shoulders and knees covered. Many provide sarongs at entry (₹30,000 deposit), but bringing your own is easier. Women on their period are traditionally not permitted to enter Hindu temples — this is still enforced at some locations.

## Footwear

You'll remove shoes constantly in Bali — temples, some restaurants, spas, guesthouses, yoga studios. Pack slip-on shoes.

- [ ] **Sandals or flip-flops** (your primary footwear — locals live in these)
- [ ] **Lightweight sneakers** (for hiking rice terraces, Ubud walks, day trips)
- [ ] **Water shoes** (optional but useful for rocky beaches and river crossings)

---

## Documents & Money

- [ ] **Passport** (valid 6+ months, required for Indonesia)
- [ ] **Visa** (60-day visa-on-arrival: $35 USD at the airport, or free 30-day stamp)
- [ ] **Travel insurance** documents (print a copy)
- [ ] **COVID vaccination card** (Indonesia no longer requires it as of 2024, but carry it)
- [ ] **Driver's license** (if renting a scooter — international permit recommended but often not checked)
- [ ] **Cash** (Indonesian Rupiah — ATMs common but carry ₹500,000 for first day)
- [ ] **Credit card** (Visa/Mastercard widely accepted, Amex less so)

---

## Electronics & Connectivity

- [ ] **Phone + charger** (Indonesia uses Type C/F plugs, 220V — bring an adapter)
- [ ] **Power bank** (10,000mAh minimum — you'll use your phone heavily)
- [ ] **Universal adapter** (Type C/F)
- [ ] **Headphones** (for flights, long van rides)
- [ ] **Camera** (optional — phone cameras are excellent, but Bali rewards a good lens)
- [ ] **E-reader** (beach reading, long afternoons by the pool)

### SIM Card
Buy a local **Telkomsel** SIM at the airport (₹150,000 for 30GB/month). 4G coverage is excellent across Bali. Download offline maps before arriving.

---

## Health & Safety

- [ ] **Sunscreen SPF 50+** (reef-safe: no oxybenzone or octinoxate)
- [ ] **After-sun lotion or aloe vera gel**
- [ ] **Insect repellent** (DEET 30%+ for dengue mosquito protection)
- [ ] **Anti-diarrheal medication** (Imodium — "Bali belly" is real)
- [ ] **Rehydration salts** (available at Circle K/Alfamart but bring a few packets)
- [ ] **Basic first-aid kit** (bandaids, antiseptic, blister treatment)
- [ ] **Prescription medications** (bring 2x what you need + a copy of prescriptions)
- [ ] **Contraception** (not always easy to find in Bali)
- [ ] **Anti-malarial** (not needed for Bali specifically, but recommended for eastern Indonesia)

### Vaccinations
Recommended: Hepatitis A, Typhoid, Tetanus. Not required but smart if you're eating street food (which you should).

## Toiletries

Most hotels provide basic toiletries. Drugstores (Guardian, Kimia Farma) are everywhere.

- [ ] **Toothbrush + toothpaste**
- [ ] **Shampoo/conditioner** (bring travel sizes, buy locally if staying longer)
- [ ] **Soap/body wash**
- [ ] **Deodorant** (clinical-strength — you'll need it in 90% humidity)
- [ ] **Razor + shaving cream**
- [ ] **Feminine hygiene products** (available locally but bring preferred brands)
- [ ] **Contact lens solution** (if you wear lenses)
- [ ] **Lip balm with SPF**
- [ ] **Hand sanitizer** (not all warungs have handwashing stations)
- [ ] **Quick-dry travel towel** (see essentials above)

## Day Pack Essentials (What to Carry Daily)

- [ ] **Reusable water bottle** (refill at hotels — Bali has a plastic problem, don't add to it)
- [ ] **Sunglasses + case**
- [ ] **Hat or cap** (sun protection)
- [ ] **Sarong** (temple visits, beach cover-up)
- [ ] **Sunscreen stick** (reapply every 2 hours)
- [ ] **Insect repellent**
- [ ] **Small dry bag** (for beach/waterfall trips — protects phone/wallet)
- [ ] **Tissues/toilet paper** (many public toilets don't provide it)
- [ ] **Hand sanitizer**
- [ ] **Cash** (₹200,000-300,000 for a full day)

---

## Things NOT to Pack

- **Bulky beach towels** — hotels provide them
- **Heavy jeans** — you'll never wear them in the heat
- **Expensive jewelry** — unnecessary and risky
- **Formal shoes** — you won't need them
- **Books** — bring an e-reader instead
- **Hairdryer** — most hotels have them

---

## Packing Tips for Bali

1. **Pack light** — you can do laundry for ₹15,000-25,000/kilo at any hotel or laundry service
2. **Bring an empty tote bag** — for beach days, market shopping, carrying temple shoes
3. **Wear your bulkiest items on the plane** (sneakers, jacket) to save luggage space
4. **Roll clothes, don't fold** — saves space and reduces wrinkles
5. **Use packing cubes** — humidity makes finding things in a messy bag harder
6. **Leave space for souvenirs** — Ubud's craft markets will tempt you
