# 3 Days in Rome: The Perfect Ancient City Itinerary

Rome's founding myth involves two brothers, a wolf, and a river. Its history spans two and a half millennia. Its coffee is excellent and its gelato is legitimately life-changing. Three days is an outrageously small amount of time for a city with more UNESCO sites than any country on Earth — but it's enough to understand why people spend their lives coming back.

## Day 1: Ancient Rome — Colosseum, Roman Forum, Palatine Hill

### Morning (8:30 AM – 1:00 PM)

Buy the combined **Colosseum + Roman Forum + Palatine Hill** ticket (€18–24 depending on season, book online weeks ahead) and arrive at opening. The **Colosseum** held 80,000 spectators for gladiatorial combat, executions, and animal hunts from 70–80 CE. The stadium is so structurally elegant — its three-tiered arcade system, the sloped seating, the underground *hypogeum* where animals and condemned prisoners waited — that modern sports architects still study it. Budget 90 minutes including the underground and arena floor access (requires a separate add-on, worth it).

Walk next door into the **Roman Forum** — the civic center of the Republic and Empire, now a field of marble column stumps and ruined basilicas that makes more sense with a map or audio guide (download the official one). Walk to the far end of the Forum and climb **Palatine Hill** — the mythological founding site of Rome and the location of the Imperial palaces. The views down into the Circus Maximus are better than anything you'll get from ground level.

**Coffee rule:** Never order a cappuccino after 10 AM in Rome — it's considered a morning-only drink. Espresso stands at any bar cost €1–1.20; stand at the counter (sitting costs 2-3x more).

### Afternoon (1:00 PM – 7:00 PM)

Lunch near the Colosseum is a tourist-trap zone — walk 10 minutes southwest to the **Testaccio neighbourhood**, Rome's historic working-class market district. **Mercato Testaccio** is a covered market with excellent prepared food stalls: *supplì* (fried rice balls with mozzarella, €2 each), thin-crust pizza by the slice (*pizza al taglio*, €2–4/100g), and the market's most famous vendor for *cacio e pepe* pasta (€6–8).

Walk the short climb up **Aventine Hill** to the **Knights of Malta Keyhole** — an iron garden door through whose keyhole you can see a perfectly framed view of St. Peter's Basilica dome, centred in the arch of hedgerows, 3 kilometres away. Ten seconds of visual magic that most Rome visitors miss entirely. Then walk to the **Orange Garden** (*Giardino degli Aranci*) on the hillside for the best 360-degree view of Rome over the rooftops.

Spend the afternoon in **Trastevere** — Rome's most charming neighbourhood, across the Tiber River from the ancient centre. Medieval streets, laundry between buildings, cats on doorsteps, and the beautiful 12th-century **Basilica di Santa Maria in Trastevere** with its Byzantine apse mosaics. Wander without agenda for 2 hours.

### Evening (7:00 PM – late)

Dinner in Trastevere: **Da Enzo al 29** on Via dei Vascellari — a classic Roman trattoria, cash only, handwritten daily menu, consistently excellent *carbonara* and *cacio e pepe* for €12–14. Queue from 7 PM. Or **Tonnarello** on Via della Paglia for outdoor dining in the medieval square with the same Roman classics at slightly higher prices.

Roman rule: *carbonara* is made with egg yolk, guanciale, pecorino, and black pepper. No cream. If a restaurant uses cream, leave.

Walk the **Gianicolo** — the long hill above Trastevere — after dinner for night views over the entire city. The viewpoint at the top looks directly north over Rome and the dome of St. Peter's floats in the orange haze.

## Day 2: Vatican and Renaissance Rome

### Morning (8:00 AM – 2:00 PM)

Book **Vatican Museums + Sistine Chapel** tickets in advance (€20 + €8 booking fee) for 8 AM entry — without this, you queue for 2-3 hours. The Vatican Museums are vast and 90% of visitors rush directly to the Sistine Chapel without stopping. Don't. The **Gallery of Maps** (16th-century painted topographical maps of all of Italy across a 120-metre corridor) and the **Raphael Rooms** (four rooms painted entirely by Raphael for Pope Julius II, 1508-1524) are each individually better than most entire museums. Budget 3 hours.

The **Sistine Chapel**: the ceiling took Michelangelo four years (1508-1512), lying on scaffolding, painting above his head. The Creation of Adam is the centrepiece but look at the prophets in the corners — the scale and the physical drama of them is what the photographs never convey. No photos, no talking — it's enforced with varying enthusiasm.

From the Vatican Museums, cross through St. Peter's Square into **St. Peter's Basilica** (free) — the largest church in Christianity, built above the tomb of St. Peter. Take the stairs (free) or elevator (€8) to the **roof** for close-up views of Bernini's colonnade and the dome exterior, then climb further to the **dome lantern** for the panoramic view of Rome. Budget 2 hours.

### Afternoon (2:00 PM – 7:00 PM)

Lunch in **Prati** — the residential neighbourhood immediately north of the Vatican, where the tourist crowds thin quickly: sandwiches from a *rosticceria* counter for €4–6, local bar food, unpretentious pizza al taglio.

Cross the Tiber to visit **Castel Sant'Angelo** (€15) — originally built as Hadrian's mausoleum in 123 CE, later converted into a fortress, papal residence, and prison. The spiral ramp inside the cylindrical tower is original Roman engineering. The terrace at the top has views over the Tiber and St. Peter's dome that don't appear in most Rome guidebooks.

Walk east along the Tiber to the **Piazza Navona** — Rome's most theatrical square, built over a 1st-century CE stadium (the oval shape gives it away). Bernini's **Fountain of the Four Rivers** at the centre is the Renaissance sculptor at his most dramatic. The square is always full of cafés and artists; skip the cafés on the square itself (€6 coffee) and go one street back.

From Navona, walk four minutes east to the **Pantheon** (€5 entry since 2022) — built by Agrippa, rebuilt by Hadrian around 125 CE, and still the best-preserved ancient building in the world. The unreinforced concrete dome (43-metre diameter) was the largest in the world for 1,300 years. The oculus — the open circular hole at the top — lets in the only light. Rain falls through it and drains through holes in the floor. Stand directly under it and tilt your head back.

Afternoon coffee at **Sant'Eustachio il Caffè** near the Pantheon — Rome's most famous espresso, *gran caffè* served with whipped sugar foam, €1.50 at the bar. The recipe is a trade secret.

### Evening (7:30 PM – late)

**Fontana di Trevi** — see it now, at night, when the day crowds have thinned and the floodlights turn the Baroque marble golden. The Trevi Fountain was built in 1762 by Nicola Salvi as the terminus of an ancient aqueduct. Throw your coin over your left shoulder with your right hand (one coin = you'll return to Rome, two = you'll fall in love there). The ritual earns the city €1.4 million per year, donated to charity.

Dinner in the **Campo de' Fiori** area: **Roscioli** on Via dei Giubbonari is a wine shop, deli counter, and restaurant combined — Roman classics with Italian wines by the glass selected by knowledgeable staff. Book ahead. €40–50 per person.

## Day 3: Baroque Masterpieces and Neighbourhood Rome

### Morning (9:00 AM – 1:00 PM)

**Borghese Gallery** (€15 + €2 booking fee, timed-entry slot required) — the most selective art museum in Rome. The ground floor sculpture rooms contain Bernini's greatest work: *Apollo and Daphne* (1622-1625), where Daphne's transformation into a laurel tree — fingers becoming leaves, feet taking root — is carved so precisely in marble that the physics of transformation appear physically real. *Pluto and Persephone*, *David* — each room delivers something extraordinary. Book the first 9 AM slot; the museum limits visitors strictly.

Walk across the **Villa Borghese Gardens** (the largest park in Rome's centre) for 45 minutes toward the **Pincian Hill Terrace** — a wide promenade overlooking Piazza del Popolo and north Rome from the park edge. Free and excellent.

### Afternoon (1:00 PM – 6:00 PM)

Lunch in **Pigneto** — a residential neighbourhood east of Termini, far from tourists, full of Roman workers: *trattorie* with handwritten menus, lunch specials of pasta and a glass of house wine for €10–12, the city's best *gricia* (the ancestor of carbonara — guanciale and pecorino, no egg) at **Primo al Pigneto** on Via del Pigneto.

Return to the **Spanish Steps** (Piazza di Spagna) in the afternoon — the 135 steps were built in 1725 connecting the French church at the top to the piazza at the bottom. The steps are always full of people sitting; join them. The view down Via Condotti (Gucci, Bulgari, Hermès) toward the Tiber is classic Rome. Gelato from **Venchi** at the bottom of the steps: nocciola (hazelnut) and gianduia (chocolate-hazelnut cream), €3–4.

**Afternoon church crawl**: Rome's Baroque churches are free, always open, and contain works that would be the highlight of any other museum: **Santa Maria della Vittoria** (Bernini's *Ecstasy of Saint Teresa*, possibly the most erotic religious sculpture ever made), **San Luigi dei Francesi** (Caravaggio's three St. Matthew paintings), **Santa Maria sopra Minerva** (Michelangelo's Christ the Redeemer, Lippi frescoes). Budget 90 minutes total, no tickets required.

### Evening

Final dinner at **Flavio al Velavevodetto** in Testaccio — the quintessential Roman neighbourhood trattoria, cut into the Monte Testaccio (a hill made from 53 million broken ancient wine amphora). *Coda alla vaccinara* (oxtail stew), *trippa alla Romana* (tripe), and *rigatoni con pajata* (intestine pasta — a Roman speciality the menu describes as "offal" and which tastes far better than the description). Cash only, no reservations for smaller parties, €30–35 per person.

## Getting There & Around

Fly into Rome Fiumicino (FCO) — the Leonardo Express train connects to Termini Station (€14, 30 minutes). Trains depart every 15 minutes from 6:23 AM to 11:23 PM. Within Rome, the city centre is walkable between most attractions; Metro Line A connects Termini to the Vatican (Ottaviano stop) and Spanish Steps (Spagna stop). Taxis are metered; Uber operates in Rome but taxis are similarly priced.
