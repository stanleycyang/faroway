# 3 Days in Barcelona: The Perfect City Itinerary

Barcelona is a city that makes it very easy to have a good time. The architecture is spectacular, the food is excellent at every price point, the beaches are right there, and nobody eats before 9 PM — which means you can arrive late from wherever you've been and still have a full evening ahead of you. Three days covers the essential Gaudí, the Gothic Quarter, and enough of the beach to justify the tan.

## Day 1: Gaudí, the Gothic Quarter, and La Barceloneta

### Morning (9:00 AM – 1:00 PM)

Start with **Sagrada Família** — book online (€26 basic, €36 with tower access) and arrive at opening to miss the worst crowds. Antoni Gaudí's basilica has been under construction since 1882 and is genuinely unlike anything else built by human hands. The exterior's two facades (the older Nativity façade and the newer Passion façade) tell different stories in wildly different visual languages, and the interior — where Gaudí's tree-column structural system creates a forest of stone — is one of the great interior spaces of the last century. Budget 2 hours; go to the tower.

From Sagrada Família, walk (or metro) west to **Casa Batlló** on Passeig de Gràcia (€25–35, skip-the-line tickets only). Gaudí's 1904 redesign of a plain apartment block into a flowing, scaled, ceramic-tiled sea-creature fantasy is more accessible than Sagrada Família and more intimate — you walk through the building's private apartments, see his furniture and detailing, and emerge onto a rooftop of coloured chimneys that suggest a dragon's spine. The 45-minute audio tour is genuinely good.

### Afternoon (1:00 PM – 7:00 PM)

Lunch on the **Passeig de Gràcia** stretch at any of the Eixample cafés — this is the city's most expensive boulevard so save the sit-down restaurant for tonight. Instead: grab a *bocadillo* (filled baguette) from a local bar for €3–5 and eat it walking.

Walk down La Rambla (once, for context — it's crowded and pickpocket-heavy but unavoidable) and dive into the **Gothic Quarter** (*Barri Gòtic*) immediately to the east. The medieval street grid is genuinely medieval — Roman walls, narrow passages, the **Barcelona Cathedral** (free in the morning, €9 in the afternoon, extraordinary cloister with 13 geese who've supposedly lived there since the 14th century), and the hidden **Plaça de Sant Felip Neri** where bullet holes from the Civil War are still visible on the church walls.

Walk east through the Born neighbourhood to **Palau de la Música Catalana** (€18–22 for a guided tour) — Lluís Domènech i Montaner's 1908 concert hall covered in flowers, mosaics, stained glass, and sculpture from floor to ceiling in every direction. Conceptually the opposite of Sagrada Família but equally astonishing in its own maximalist way.

Late afternoon: head south to **La Barceloneta** beach. It's urban, it's crowded, and it's genuinely enjoyable — a real city beach with volleyball nets, cold beer in plastic cups, and the Mediterranean. Swim. Take your time.

### Evening (9:00 PM – midnight)

Dinner in the **El Born** neighbourhood — Barcelona's most food-forward area. The tapas bar density around Carrer del Rec and Passeig del Born is high; skip the tourist-facing ones on the main square and go one block back. **Bar Brutal** on Carrer de la Princesa for natural wine and creative *pintxos*. Or **Sagàs** on Carrer dels Carders for proper Catalan cuisine at €30–40 per person.

Don't eat before 9 PM — restaurants before then are half-empty and service is slower. This is how Barcelona works.

## Day 2: Park Güell, Montjuïc, and the Old Harbour

### Morning (8:00 AM – 1:00 PM)

**Park Güell** (€10, book a timed entry slot) opens at 8 AM — arrive at the first slot to see the mosaic terrace before anyone else. Gaudí designed the park as a garden city housing development that was never completed; what remains is a fantasyland of ceramic benches, colonnaded market halls, and a gingerbread gatehouse. The lower sections of the park (outside the ticketed monumental zone) are free and worth walking through before heading up.

From Park Güell, walk 20 minutes south to the **Casa Milà / La Pedrera** (€25, advance ticket). Built 1906-1912 and UNESCO-listed, its stone façade undulates like a cliff face worn by the sea. The rooftop — those warrior-like chimney stacks — is the defining image. The interior apartment showing is less interesting than Batlló; the rooftop is better.

**Morning coffee:** Barcelona's best coffee is not in the tourist corridor. Look for *obrador* (bakery) signs in the Eixample streets north of Diagonal — the coffee and croissants come from the same kitchen and both are excellent. Budget €3–4.

### Afternoon (1:00 PM – 7:00 PM)

Take the metro or cable car to **Montjuïc** hill. The 1929 **National Palace** (*Museu Nacional d'Art de Catalunya*, MNAC) contains the world's finest collection of Romanesque art — frescoes removed from Pyrenean churches in the 1920s (controversially, but now safeguarded) that are extraordinary in person. Entry €12. The terrace in front of the palace has the best panoramic view of Barcelona's grid stretching to the sea.

Walk downhill through the **Olympic Stadium** (Barcelona '92 is recent enough that locals still feel warmth toward it) and continue to the **Poble Sec** neighbourhood at the base of the hill — a working-class area that's become Barcelona's most interesting food destination, particularly along **Carrer de Blai** (Pintxos Street): 20+ bars serving Basque-style pintxos (€1.50–2.50 each) that constitute the best value lunch in the city. Arrive between 1–2 PM for freshly made rounds.

Spend the late afternoon in the **Parc de la Ciutadella** near El Born — the city's main park, with a monumental waterfall fountain built by Gaudí (as a student, before Sagrada Família, as a side note) and a pleasant lake with rowboat rentals (€6/30 min).

### Evening (8:00 PM – late)

Dinner in **Poble Sec** or the **Raval** neighbourhood: **Bodega Cañete** on Carrer Unió for classic Catalan food in a basement wine bodega (€40–50/person); or **La Mar Salada** near the port for seafood *paella* and Catalan fish stew (*suquet*) that rivals anything in the tourist-facing restaurants for half the price.

Drinks along the **Passeig del Born** waterfront after dinner — a wide promenade lined with chairs and bars that stays busy until 2 AM. The Barcelonian night schedule runs about 2 hours later than the rest of Europe.

## Day 3: Markets, Montserrat Day Trip, and Final Tapas

### Morning (8:00 AM – 2:00 PM)

**Mercat de Sant Josep de la Boqueria** on La Rambla opens at 8 AM — go early before the tourist crowds arrive and it becomes primarily a photo opportunity. The market is genuinely great: whole *jamón ibérico* legs hanging from every stall, 12 varieties of olives by the scoop, fresh juice bars (*granissada* de meló for €1.50), and the fish market section in the back where Barcelona's restaurant buyers come at 6 AM. Breakfast at one of the market bars: *pa amb tomàquet* (Catalan tomato bread) with cheese for €4.

Option A — **Montserrat Day Trip** (75 minutes by train + rack railway from Plaça d'Espanya): the mountain monastery perched on extraordinary serrated rock formations above the Barcelona plain. The hike to Sant Joan peak (1,200m) takes 2 hours round trip and the views of the Pyrenees and coastline are unforgettable. The Black Madonna inside the basilica is one of the most venerated religious objects in Catalonia. Take the 9 AM train and return by 2 PM.

Option B — Stay in Barcelona: **Museu Picasso** in El Born (€14, free on Thursday evenings) — the most important Picasso collection in the world, housed in five connected medieval mansions, covering his early Barcelona years in extraordinary detail.

### Afternoon (2:00 PM – 7:00 PM)

Spend the afternoon in **Barceloneta** or along the **Olympic Port** — have a long lunch by the sea (*arrós a banda* is the Valencian-style seafood rice to order here), walk the coastal promenade north toward **Poblenou** if you have energy, and spend the late afternoon doing the thing you didn't get to in the first two days.

**Shopping:** The streets east of Passeig de Gràcia — particularly **Carrer d'Enric Granados** (traffic-free boulevard with parasols) and the **Eixample grid** — have Barcelona's best independent shops, from local fashion labels to specialist food stores for Catalan *cava* and olive oil.

### Evening (from 8:00 PM)

Final dinner at **Tickets** (Albert Adrià's tapas bar, book 2 months in advance, €60–80/person) if you planned ahead — the most creative tapas menu in the city. Or **Bodega 1900** from the same group, slightly easier to book, serving updated versions of traditional Spanish vermouth bar dishes in a gorgeous 1900s setting.

Walk from dinner through the Gothic Quarter one last time as the shops close and the lanes quiet down — this is when the neighbourhood reveals the city it actually is.

## Getting There & Around

Fly into Barcelona El Prat Airport (BCN) — the Aerobús runs every 5 minutes to Plaça Catalunya (€6.75, 35 minutes). Within the city, the Metro runs until midnight (2 AM weekends) and covers everything you need: buy a **T-Casual** 10-trip card (€11.35) at any station machine.
