# Bangkok Food Guide: What to Eat, Where & How Much

Bangkok is one of the world's great food cities — not because of its restaurants (though it has excellent ones) but because the best food happens at street level, cooked in woks over charcoal flames by vendors who've been making the same three dishes for 30 years. You can eat spectacularly well here for $5. You can also eat spectacularly well for $200. Here's how to navigate both.

---

## Bangkok Food Culture Overview

Thai food is built on four fundamental flavors that appear in nearly every dish: spicy (*phet*), sour (*priao*), sweet (*wan*), and salty (*kem*). The best dishes balance all four simultaneously — *tom yum* soup, *som tam* papaya salad, *pad krapow* holy basil stir-fry. Meals are communal (order several dishes and share), and eating alone is so uncommon that solo diners ordering full meals sometimes draw concerned questions from staff.

**Key cultural rules:**
- Thais eat with a spoon (right hand) and fork (left hand for pushing food onto the spoon). Chopsticks are for noodle soups only.
- Rice (*khao*) is considered sacred — wasting it is rude.
- Tipping is uncommon at street stalls and local restaurants (10% at upscale places only).
- Spice tolerance varies — always ask for "*phet nit noi*" (a little spicy) unless you're confident.

## Must-Try Dishes

### 1. **Pad Thai** — Stir-Fried Rice Noodles
The tourist cliché that's actually excellent when done right. Thin rice noodles stir-fried with egg, tofu, dried shrimp, bean sprouts, peanuts, and tamarind sauce. The best versions come from carts that do nothing else. Look for the *wok hei* (breath of the wok) — that slightly charred flavor that only comes from high heat and quick cooking.

**Where:** Thip Samai on Maha Chai Road (the Michelin-recognized Pad Thai institution, ฿100-200)  
**Cost:** ฿50-80 at street stalls, ฿100-200 at restaurants

### 2. **Tom Yum Goong** — Spicy Shrimp Soup
Hot and sour soup with prawns, lemongrass, galangal, kaffir lime leaves, and Thai chilies. The broth should be cloudy from the evaporated milk or coconut cream, intensely aromatic, and balanced between sour and spicy. One of Thailand's national dishes and exported globally (rarely as well).

**Where:** P'Aor Tom Yum Kung on Maha Chai Road (near the Grand Palace, beloved by locals)  
**Cost:** ฿120-180 per bowl

### 3. **Som Tam** — Green Papaya Salad
Shredded unripe papaya pounded in a mortar with garlic, chilies, tomatoes, long beans, dried shrimp, peanuts, lime juice, and fish sauce. Technically from northeastern Thailand (*Isaan*) but ubiquitous in Bangkok. Order it "*mai sai poo*" (without fermented crab) unless you're adventurous — the crab adds funk.

**Where:** Any Isaan restaurant, or Som Tam Nua on Siam Square (excellent, ฿80-100)  
**Cost:** ฿40-80

### 4. **Khao Pad** — Fried Rice
Thai fried rice with jasmine rice, egg, onion, tomato, and your choice of protein. Simple, ubiquitous, and when made well (high heat, day-old rice, good *wok hei*) it's profoundly satisfying. Often served with sliced cucumber and a wedge of lime.

**Where:** Literally anywhere, but **Jay Fai's** crab omelette fried rice is legendary (฿1,000, 2-hour queue, Michelin star)  
**Cost:** ฿60-100 standard, ฿300-1,000 at Jay Fai

### 5. **Massaman Curry** — Rich Coconut Curry
The mildest of Thai curries — influenced by Persian and Indian Muslim traders who arrived centuries ago. Slow-cooked beef or chicken in coconut milk with potatoes, peanuts, bay leaves, cardamom, and tamarind. Sweet, savory, slightly sour, and deeply comforting.

**Where:** Muslim Restaurant on Charoen Krung Road (one of the oldest, ฿120)  
**Cost:** ฿80-150

### 6. **Pad Krapow Moo** — Holy Basil Stir-Fry
Minced pork (or chicken) stir-fried with holy basil, garlic, chilies, and fish sauce, served over rice with a fried egg (*khai dao*). This is Bangkok office workers' lunch — fast, cheap, spicy, and addictive. The holy basil (*krapow*) is key — it has a peppery, slightly anise flavor that Thai basil lacks.

**Where:** Any streetside stall with a wok and a crowd of locals at noon  
**Cost:** ฿60-80

## Street Food Scene

Bangkok's street food operates on a scale that makes most cities' food truck scenes look amateur. The best areas:

### **Yaowarat (Chinatown)**
The single best street food neighbourhood in Bangkok. Come after dark when the cooking carts take over Yaowarat Road. Walk slowly, point at things, eat everything.

**Essential stops:**
- **Nai Mong Hoi Tod** — crispy oyster omelettes (฿80)
- **T&K Seafood** — massive grilled prawns and crab (฿200-500)
- **Jok Prince** — rice porridge (*jok*) with pork meatballs (฿60)
- **Pa Tong Go Savoey** — fried dough (*pa tong go*) with warm soy milk (฿35)

### **Victory Monument**
The circle around Victory Monument has the densest concentration of street food vendors per square metre in the city. The breakfast *khao tom* (rice soup) stalls open at 5 AM; the grilled meat stalls don't close until 3 AM. Famous for *boat noodles* (*kuay teaw reua*) — tiny bowls of intensely flavoured broth served 5-6 at a time for ฿10-15 each.

### **Or Tor Kor Market**
Bangkok's highest-quality produce market, directly across from Chatuchak. The prepared food section is where upscale Bangkok goes for lunch — *khao niaow ma muang* (mango sticky rice, ฿100), grilled fish, *khanom jeen* (fermented rice noodles with curry, ฿80).

## Restaurants by Budget

### Budget (under ฿150/meal)
Bangkok's "cheap eats" are legitimately excellent at scale. Any street stall or *raan khao gaeng* (curry-over-rice shop) with a lunch queue of locals is safe.

**Recommended:**
- **Khao Gaeng** stalls — point at 3-4 curries/stir-fries from the display, get them over rice (฿60-80)
- **Noodle shops** — *kuay teaw* (noodle soup, ฿50-80) or *ba mee* (egg noodles, ฿60)
- **Muslim restaurants** in the Samyan or Charoen Krung areas — *roti*, biryani, *murtabak* (filled flatbread, ฿80-120)

### Mid-range (฿150-400/meal)
The sweet spot — sit-down restaurants, air conditioning, English menus, still excellent value.

**Recommended:**
- **Som Tam Nua** (Siam Square) — Isaan food, ฿100-150 per dish
- **Krua Apsorn** (near Democracy Monument) — royal Thai cuisine, ฿150-250 per dish
- **Baan Phadthai** — elevated Pad Thai in a beautiful shophouse (฿180-280)
- **Sorn** (if you can book) — two Michelin stars, southern Thai cuisine, ฿3,500 tasting menu

### Splurge (฿400+/meal)
Bangkok has more Michelin stars than any Southeast Asian city and some of the region's most ambitious restaurants.

**Recommended:**
- **Gaggan Anand** — progressive Indian, 2 Michelin stars, ฿6,500+ tasting menu (book months ahead)
- **Le Du** — modern Thai, 2 Michelin stars, ฿3,900 menu
- **80/20** — Thai fine dining with local ingredients, 1 Michelin star, ฿2,500
- **Jay Fai** — street food legend, 1 Michelin star, crab omelette ฿1,000 (queue 2 hours)

## Vegetarian & Vegan Options

Thai cuisine is vegetarian-friendly if you know how to order. The phrase "*jeh*" or "*jay*" means strict vegan (no animal products, no garlic/onion in Buddhist interpretation).

**Where:**
- **May Kaidee** (multiple locations) — Thai vegan institution, excellent curries (฿120-180)
- **Broccoli Revolution** (Sukhumvit) — plant-based comfort food (฿150-250)
- **Chatuchak Market** — entire section of *jeh* food stalls (Section 26)

## Food Markets & Neighbourhoods

### **Ari Neighbourhood**
Bangkok's most liveable neighbourhood has the city's best concentration of Japanese curry shops, craft coffee, and casual Thai restaurants where actual Thais eat. Less touristy than Thonglor, better food than Sukhumvit.

**Must-visit:** **Aroi** for green curry (฿120)

### **Pigneto / Ekkamai**
The hipster/creative class neighbourhoods — excellent craft beer bars, Italian restaurants, and some of Bangkok's best *khao tom* (rice soup) late-night stalls.

### **Khlong Toei Market**
Bangkok's largest wet market — not for the squeamish. Fish, meat, and produce under a highway overpass where the city's restaurant buyers come at dawn. Unfiltered Bangkok.

## Dining Etiquette

- **Share everything** — Thai meals are communal. Order 3-4 dishes for 2 people.
- **Don't use chopsticks for rice** — spoon and fork only.
- **Tap water is not safe** — drink bottled water or order "*nam plao*" (plain water) from vendors.
- **No shoes in some restaurants** — traditional Thai and Japanese places enforce this.
- **Eat the garnish** — the cucumber and cabbage on your *som tam* plate are meant to be eaten to cool the spice.
