# 3 Days in Kyoto: The Perfect Cultural Itinerary

Japan's ancient capital held the Imperial Court for over a thousand years, and it shows — 17 UNESCO World Heritage sites, 1,600 Buddhist temples, 400 Shinto shrines, and a pace of life so intentionally slow it feels like a form of protest. Kyoto is everything Tokyo isn't. Three days barely scratches the surface. Here's where to scratch.

## Day 1: Classic Kyoto — Fushimi, Gion, Pontocho

### Morning (7:30 AM – 12:00 PM)

Go to **Fushimi Inari Taisha** before 8 AM and the thousands of vermillion torii gates will be nearly empty. This is the most important thing you'll do in Kyoto — not because the shrine at the base is spectacular (it's fine) but because the hike up Mt. Inari through an unbroken tunnel of gates stretching for 4 kilometres is genuinely otherworldly. You don't need to go all the way to the summit (2 hours roundtrip) — 45 minutes up to the Yotsutsuji intersection gives you the iconic density of gates plus a view over the city, then turn back. Free admission.

Take the JR Nara Line two stops north back to Kyoto Station, then bus to **Nijo Castle** (¥1,030). Built in 1603 for Tokugawa Ieyasu, it's the finest example of flatland castle architecture in Japan — the "nightingale floors" creak deliberately to alert guards to anyone walking, and the painted sliding doors inside (Kano school, 16th century) are some of the most beautiful things in the country. Budget 90 minutes.

**Morning fuel:** Stop at a Nishiki Market stall before heading south — try *yuba* (tofu skin) skewers and *tamagoyaki* rolled omelette. The market opens at 9 AM, located on a covered arcade one block north of Shijo Street.

### Afternoon (12:00 PM – 6:00 PM)

Spend the afternoon in **Gion** — Kyoto's famous geisha district. The neighbourhood is best experienced slowly on foot: wander the stone-paved lanes of **Shirakawa-minami-dori** along the canal, and the preserved machiya townhouses of **Hanamikoji Street** where, if you're very lucky and it's the right hour, you might glimpse a *maiko* (apprentice geisha) walking to an evening appointment. Don't photograph them without permission; it's considered deeply rude.

From Gion, walk 15 minutes east to **Kiyomizudera** Temple — perched on a hillside on wooden pillars without a single nail, it overlooks the entire city. The terrace view at golden hour is extraordinary. Allow 90 minutes, and walk the approach street (Ninenzaka and Sannenzaka) lined with Edo-period shops selling Kyoto crafts, ceramics, and *yatsuhashi* (cinnamon rice cakes).

### Evening (6:00 PM – 10:00 PM)

Dinner on **Pontocho** — a narrow lantern-lit alley between Gion and the Kamo River, stuffed with restaurants of every price point, all competing intensely for quality. Sit at a place with *noren* (hanging fabric) over the door and a handwritten menu — those are the good ones. Look for *obanzai* (Kyoto-style small plates): simmered tofu, pickled vegetables, and seasonal fish that change daily. Budget ¥2,500–4,000 per person.

After dinner, walk to the **Kamo River** — locals sit along the banks in couples and groups, talking quietly. In warmer months, restaurant platforms extend over the water (*kawayuka*). The entire stretch between Sanjo and Shijo bridges is one of the great urban promenades in Asia.

## Day 2: Zen and Bamboo — Arashiyama and the Mountain Temples

### Morning (8:00 AM – 1:00 PM)

Take the Sagano Scenic Railway (or a 30-minute bus) to **Arashiyama** in the western foothills. Arrive before 9 AM and walk directly to the **Arashiyama Bamboo Grove** — the narrow path between walls of towering green bamboo is genuinely magical in early morning light when the tourist waves haven't arrived. It takes 10 minutes to walk through; that's not the point. The point is standing still and listening.

From the bamboo grove, walk five minutes to **Tenryu-ji**, a Rinzai Zen temple with a 14th-century landscape garden designed by Muso Soseki. The garden pond reflects the bamboo hills behind it in a composition that's been here since 1344. Spend 45 minutes. Entry: ¥500 for the garden.

Rent a **rowboat** on the Oi River from the boathouse near Togetsukyo Bridge (¥1,500/hour) and drift under the mountains for an hour — one of the most peaceful activities in all of Japan.

**Arashiyama lunch:** The noodle shops along the main street serve *tofu ramen* and *matcha soba* — both regional specialties. **Shigetsu** inside Tenryu-ji serves shojin ryori (Buddhist vegetarian cuisine) for ¥3,500 — book ahead.

### Afternoon (1:00 PM – 6:00 PM)

Walk 20 minutes north from central Arashiyama into the forested hills to reach the **Sagano area** and the smaller, far less visited temples: **Jojakko-ji**, a moss-covered hillside temple with a 5-storey pagoda visible above the trees; and **Nison-in**, where in autumn the maple-lined approach turns entirely red. These two temples see a tenth of the visitors of the famous sites and are twice as beautiful in their way.

Return to central Kyoto by late afternoon and walk the **Philosopher's Path** — a 2-kilometre canal-side walkway lined with cherry trees connecting Ginkaku-ji (Silver Pavilion) in the north to Nanzen-ji in the south. In late March the entire path is a tunnel of pink; in other seasons it's simply a beautiful walk with good coffee shops at intervals.

### Evening (6:00 PM – late)

**Nishiki Market** after dark for street food, then dinner in the covered shopping arcade area near Shijo-Karasuma. For something special: book **Tosuiro**, a Kyoto kaiseki restaurant focused on *tofu* in all its forms — yuba, agedashi, dengaku — where a full course costs ¥6,000 and is the most nuanced thing you'll eat this year.

## Day 3: North Kyoto and the Imperial Villa

### Morning (8:30 AM – 12:30 PM)

Reserve ahead for the **Kinkaku-ji** (Golden Pavilion) opening at 9 AM — the gold-leafed Zen temple reflected in its mirror pond is one of those images you've seen so many times it barely seems real until you're standing in front of it. Arrive exactly at opening to beat the midday crowds. Budget 45 minutes, then move immediately to...

**Ryoan-ji**, 15 minutes walk west: the world's most famous rock garden — 15 stones arranged in raked white gravel, the composition designed so that from any angle, one stone is always hidden. Sit on the viewing platform for 15 minutes and contemplate it properly. Most visitors photograph and leave in 3 minutes. Don't be those visitors.

### Afternoon (12:30 PM – 5:30 PM)

Lunch at **Nishiki Market** — graze your way through: grilled *mochi*, pickled vegetables from Kyoto's famous *tsukemono* shops, fresh *wagashi* sweets made while you watch.

Spend the afternoon at the **Fushimi Sake District** in southern Kyoto — the historic brewery quarter that produces 30% of Japan's sake. Walk Sakazuki-cho Street past 40+ breweries with their cedar ball decorations (*sugidama*) hanging over the doors. Most offer tastings for ¥200-500 per cup. **Gekkeikan Okura Sake Museum** (¥600) has the full history and excellent tasting flights.

### Evening (5:30 PM onwards)

Return to Gion for the best possible farewell: walk Hanamikoji at dusk when the lanterns come on, have a final bowl of *ramen* or *udon* at a low-key counter restaurant, and spend a last hour in the covered Nishiki Market arcade buying *yatsuhashi* sweets, quality chopsticks, and matcha to take home.

## Getting There & Around

Kyoto sits on the Tokaido Shinkansen (bullet train) line — from Tokyo it's 2 hours 20 minutes by Nozomi train (¥13,850 reserved). Buy a Japan Rail Pass if you're doing Tokyo + Kyoto + anywhere else; it pays off quickly. Within Kyoto, buses cover everything (¥230/ride or ¥700 for a day pass). Taxis are metered and run about ¥1,000 for most crosstown trips.
