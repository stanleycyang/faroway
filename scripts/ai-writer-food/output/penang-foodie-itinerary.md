Three days in Penang is barely enough time to scratch the surface, but it's enough to eat extraordinarily well if you're strategic. This is the itinerary I built meal by meal, walking off one dish while hunting the next, learning which stalls open when, and figuring out how to eat six times a day without collapsing. Follow this route and you'll taste Penang properly — the hawker centers at their loudest, the quiet kopitiam mornings, and everything between.

## Before You Eat: Quick Notes

Penang runs on cash — bring plenty of Malaysian Ringgit (MYR), especially for hawker stalls and kopitiams. Most street food vendors don't take cards. Meals here start early: breakfast stalls open by 7am, lunch crowds peak at noon, and some legendary spots sell out by 2pm. Tipping isn't expected at hawker centers, but rounding up at restaurants is appreciated. No reservations needed for street food, but book ahead for fine dining on weekends. Learn "terima kasih" (thank you) — it goes far.

## Day 1: George Town's Greatest Hits

**Morning — Char Kway Teow at Its Source**

Start in the Old Town, where the day begins quietly before the heat sets in. Your first meal should be Penang's most iconic: char kway teow. Find a corner kopitiam where an aunty works a single wok over roaring flames, tossing flat rice noodles with prawns, cockles, egg, bean sprouts, and lard cracklings. The best versions have *wok hei* — that elusive smoky char you can only achieve with a seasoned wok and decades of practice. The noodles should glisten, not swim in oil. Pair it with thick local coffee (*kopi-o*) pulled from a sock filter. This is breakfast that announces you've arrived.

*Estimated cost: MYR 8–15 (~$2–4)*

**Afternoon — Hawker Hunting in the City Center**

By noon, head toward the City Center where the lunch rush transforms covered hawker centers into symphonies of sizzling and shouting. This is where you eat Penang's legendary *asam laksa* — the sour, fish-based noodle soup that's nothing like the coconut curries you might expect. It's tangy from tamarind, fragrant with torch ginger flower, and topped with *hae ko* (shrimp paste) that adds a funky depth. Let the flavors challenge you; this dish doesn't aim to please on first slurp, but by the third spoonful, you'll understand why UNESCO recognized Penang as a Creative City of Gastronomy.

Spend the afternoon wandering the market stalls nearby. Graze on *rojak* (fruit and vegetable salad with sticky prawn paste sauce), *cendol* (shaved ice with palm sugar and coconut milk), and whatever catches your eye. The best education is eating your way through.

*Estimated cost: MYR 25–40 (~$6–10)*

**Evening — Waterfront Dinner Done Right**

As the sun drops, make your way to the Waterfront for dinner with a view. This is where Penang pivots from street food to something more composed. Choose a restaurant serving refined versions of local seafood — butter prawns with curry leaves and chili, or *har mee* (prawn noodle soup) elevated with premium ingredients. The breeze off the water, the soft lighting, the slower pace — this is Penang reminding you it's not all plastic stools and fluorescent lights. Order generously; portions are meant for sharing.

*Estimated cost: MYR 80–150 (~$18–35)*

**Day 1 Total Food Spend:** Budget ~MYR 115 (~$25) · Mid-range ~MYR 200 (~$45) · Splurge ~MYR 350 (~$80)

## Day 2: The Hidden Side of Penang

**Morning — The Kopitiam Ritual**

Today starts in the Arts Quarter, where gentrification meets stubborn tradition. Seek out an old-school kopitiam that's resisted renovation — the kind with marble-topped tables, ceiling fans working overtime, and elderly regulars reading Chinese newspapers. Order *nasi lemak*: coconut rice with sambal, fried anchovies, peanuts, cucumber, and a fried egg. The sambal should have heat but also sweetness, the rice fragrant but not greasy. Add a side of half-boiled eggs seasoned with white pepper and soy sauce. Dip your toast in them. This is how generations of Penangites have started their days.

*Estimated cost: MYR 10–18 (~$2–4)*

**Afternoon — Deep into the Neighborhoods**

Leave the tourist center behind. Head to a local neighborhood where the hawker stalls cater to residents, not visitors. This is where you find *chee cheong fun* (rice noodle rolls) with sweet sauce and shrimp paste, or *lor bak* (five-spice pork rolls) fried to shatteringly crisp. The crowds here are families, not backpackers. The aunties don't speak much English, but pointing works fine.

For the adventurous: find the off-the-beaten market tucked behind residential blocks. It's louder, messier, and more real than anything in George Town proper. This is where you taste *popiah* (fresh spring rolls) assembled to order, *kuih* (traditional sweets) in every color, and whatever seasonal fruit is piled highest. Eat standing. Eat with your hands. This is Penang at its most honest.

Consider joining a cooking class this afternoon if time allows — learning to pound a proper *rempah* (spice paste) by hand will change how you taste every curry thereafter.

*Estimated cost: MYR 30–50 (~$7–12)*

**Evening — Splurge on Heritage Cuisine**

Tonight, treat yourself. Penang's fine dining scene has grown sophisticated, with restaurants that honor traditional recipes while applying modern technique. Look for places in the Business District or restored heritage buildings serving Nyonya cuisine — the Peranakan fusion of Chinese and Malay traditions. *Ayam pongteh* (chicken braised in fermented soybean sauce), *pie tee* (crispy cups filled with jicama), and *otak-otak* (grilled fish custard in banana leaves) showcase flavors you won't find in hawker stalls. Pair with a local craft cocktail or just more teh tarik.

*Estimated cost: MYR 120–220 (~$28–50)*

**Day 2 Total Food Spend:** Budget ~MYR 160 (~$35) · Mid-range ~MYR 285 (~$65) · Splurge ~MYR 450 (~$100)

## Day 3: The Farewell Feast

**Morning — One Last Proper Breakfast**

Return to Old Town for your final morning, but hit a different spot. Today it's *hokkien mee* — prawn noodle soup with a broth so intensely shrimpy it stains the bowl orange. The best versions simmer prawn heads for hours. You'll get a bowl of noodles swimming in liquid gold, topped with pork slices, kangkung (water spinach), and fried shallots. Add chili paste if you dare. This is the dish I craved on the flight home.

Stop at a hidden cafe nearby for a second coffee and *kaya toast* — bread grilled with coconut-pandan jam and a slab of cold butter that melts slowly as you eat.

*Estimated cost: MYR 15–25 (~$4–6)*

**Afternoon — The Final Market Walk**

Your last afternoon is for filling gaps. Return to any hawker center you loved or one you missed. This is snacking time: *pasembur* (fried fritters with sweet potato sauce), *ais kacang* (shaved ice with every topping imaginable), *tau sar pneah* (mung bean pastries) to take home. Buy extra. Eat slowly. Photograph nothing — just remember.

Walk through a final neighborhood as the lunch rush fades. Watch the vendors pack up. Notice how the city exhales in the afternoon heat.

*Estimated cost: MYR 25–45 (~$6–10)*

**Evening — The Meal Worth Ending On**

Your final dinner should be the one you'd fly back for. Return to the dish that surprised you most, or seek out the secret viewpoint overlooking the city and dine nearby as the sun sets. If you haven't yet had proper Penang *curry mee* — noodles in spicy coconut curry broth with cockles, tofu puffs, and blood cubes for the brave — make it happen tonight. End with something sweet: *tau foo fah* (silken tofu pudding with ginger syrup) or a final bowl of *cendol* as the night markets light up around you.

*Estimated cost: MYR 60–150 (~$14–35)*

**Day 3 Total Food Spend:** Budget ~MYR 100 (~$22) · Mid-range ~MYR 220 (~$50) · Splurge ~MYR 400 (~$90)

## Pro Tips for Food Travelers

- **Eat early or miss out.** The best char kway teow stalls sell out by 1pm. Legendary spots operate on their own schedules — ask locals what time to arrive, then show up 15 minutes earlier.

- **Watch the queue, not the signage.** Penangites know which stall in a hawker center is worth waiting for. If one stall has a line and the neighbors don't, get in that line.

- **Dress for sweat, not style.** You'll be eating in hot, humid conditions with no air conditioning. Wear breathable clothes, bring tissues (they double as napkins), and embrace the experience.

- **Order like a local: point and gesture.** Many hawkers speak limited English but understand perfectly when you point at what you want. Hold up fingers for quantity. Smile. It works.
