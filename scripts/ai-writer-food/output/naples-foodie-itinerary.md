Naples doesn't reward the casual visitor. It rewards the obsessive — the ones willing to stand in line at 7am for a fried rice ball, to track down the pizzeria with no sign on a backstreet in the Spanish Quarter, to eat their fourth sfogliatella of the day because this bakery uses a different lard ratio. I spent three days doing exactly that, and what follows is the itinerary I wish someone had handed me before I arrived.

## Before You Eat: Quick Notes

Neapolitan kitchens run on their own clock. Lunch happens between 1pm and 3pm; dinner rarely before 8:30pm. Many serious restaurants are cash-only or strongly prefer it — carry euros. Tipping isn't expected but rounding up is appreciated. Book dinner at popular pizzerias at least a day ahead, especially weekends. Dress modestly if you're eating near churches or historic sites, and greet staff when entering — a simple "buongiorno" goes far.

## Day 1: Centro Storico — Pizza, Markets, and the Foundations

The historic center is where Naples reveals its soul through food. Narrow streets, scooters threading past, and the smell of wood-fired ovens drifting from every other doorway. Today is about anchoring yourself in the classics.

### Morning

Start at a bar in the City Center for *caffè* and *sfogliatella* — the shell-shaped pastry filled with ricotta and candied citrus that defines Neapolitan breakfast. The two styles matter: *riccia* (flaky, layered) and *frolla* (shortcrust, softer). Try the riccia first. Order your espresso at the counter, drink it in three sips, and move on. This is how Neapolitans do it.

**What to order:** Sfogliatella riccia and caffè  
**Where:** Any historic bar in Centro Storico — look for one with locals standing at the counter, not sitting  
**Cost:** €3–5

### Afternoon

Lunch is pizza, and there's no avoiding the conversation: which pizzeria? For your first Neapolitan pizza, head to a spot in the Old Town where the menu is short and the margherita is the point. The dough should be soft, slightly charred, with a cornicione (crust edge) that puffs like a pillow. San Marzano tomatoes, fior di latte, basil, olive oil. Nothing else.

After, walk through the Porta Nolana market near the Waterfront. This is Naples' oldest food market — fish still gleaming with seawater, vegetables piled in chaotic abundance, vendors shouting prices. Buy nothing or everything. Either way, you'll understand where the city's cooking begins.

Mid-afternoon, find a *friggitoria* (fried food shop) and order *cuoppo* — a paper cone filled with fried seafood, croquettes, and whatever else is coming out of the oil. Eat it walking.

**What to order:** Pizza margherita; cuoppo misto  
**Where:** Old Town pizzeria; friggitoria near Porta Nolana market  
**Cost:** Pizza €5–8; cuoppo €4–6; market snacks €3–5

### Evening

Dinner in the Centro Storico should be traditional Neapolitan home cooking — not pizza, but *ragù*. Find a trattoria with handwritten menus and order *ziti spezzati al ragù napoletano* (broken ziti with slow-cooked meat sauce). The ragù has been simmering since morning. Pair with *friarielli* (bitter broccoli rabe sautéed with garlic and chili) on the side. House wine, poured from a jug. Finish with *babà* soaked in rum — the cake that collapses sweetly under your fork.

**What to order:** Ziti al ragù, friarielli, babà al rum  
**Where:** Traditional trattoria, Centro Storico  
**Cost:** €25–45

**Day 1 Total Food Spend:** Budget ~€40 | Mid ~€55 | Splurge ~€85

## Day 2: Quartieri Spagnoli & Vomero — Street Food, Hidden Spots, and High Views

Today moves from the chaotic energy of the Spanish Quarter to the residential calm of Vomero above. The contrast is the point.

### Morning

Skip the hotel breakfast. Head into the Quartieri Spagnoli — a grid of narrow streets where laundry hangs between buildings and nonnas watch from balconies. Find a small bakery for *taralli* (crunchy, savory ring biscuits with almonds or pepper) and another espresso. This neighborhood is where Neapolitans live, not where tourists perform. Eat standing at the counter or on a church step.

For a second breakfast (yes, second — you're in Naples), find *pastiera* if it's anywhere near Easter, or *zeppole* (fried dough balls) year-round. The hidden cafes here don't advertise; they just exist.

**What to order:** Taralli, caffè, zeppole or pastiera  
**Where:** Quartieri Spagnoli bakeries and bars  
**Cost:** €4–7

### Afternoon

Lunch in the Quartieri Spagnoli should be *arancini* or *crocchè* from a street window — the fried rice balls stuffed with ragù and peas, and the potato croquettes that shatter when you bite through. This is standing-up food, eaten from wax paper, probably too hot. 

Then take the funicular up to Vomero for the view and the walk. The Arts Quarter feel here is more residential, quieter. Find a salumeria (deli) and assemble a modest lunch: aged provolone, local salami, some olives. Eat on a bench overlooking the bay. This is the €12 lunch that feeds you for hours.

Consider a pasta-making class in this area if you want to learn *gnocchi alla sorrentina* or hand-rolled *scialatielli*. Several small cooking schools operate in the residential streets — book a day ahead.

**What to order:** Arancini, crocchè; salumeria provisions  
**Where:** Street vendors in Quartieri Spagnoli; salumeria in Vomero  
**Cost:** Street food €4–6; deli lunch €10–15; cooking class €60–90 (optional)

### Evening

Dinner tonight should be seafood — you're in a port city. Head back down toward the Waterfront and find a restaurant serving *spaghetti alle vongole* (clams, garlic, white wine, parsley, no tomato) or *frittura di paranza* (mixed fried small fish). The fish should have been swimming that morning. Order *insalata di mare* to start — octopus, squid, shrimp in lemon and olive oil.

**What to order:** Insalata di mare, spaghetti alle vongole, frittura di paranza  
**Where:** Seafood restaurant, Waterfront area  
**Cost:** €35–60

**Day 2 Total Food Spend:** Budget ~€45 | Mid ~€70 | Splurge ~€130

## Day 3: Sanità & Farewell Feast — The Locals' Neighborhood and One Last Pizza

Your final day goes deeper. The Sanità district, north of Centro Storico, is where the tourist density drops and the food gets more personal. End with the best of what you've learned.

### Morning

Breakfast in Sanità. Find a pasticceria serving *graffa* — Neapolitan doughnuts, lighter than American versions, dusted in sugar. Pair with *spremuta d'arancia* (fresh-squeezed orange juice) if the season is right, or another espresso if it isn't. This neighborhood has catacombs and baroque churches, but you're here for the bakeries and the pace.

Walk through the local market here — smaller than Porta Nolana, but more intimate. Vendors know their customers by name. Buy some dried figs or *nocciole* (hazelnuts) for later.

**What to order:** Graffa, spremuta or caffè  
**Where:** Pasticceria and market in Sanità  
**Cost:** €4–7

### Afternoon

Lunch is a final market walk and strategic snacking. Return to the Centro Storico or explore near Via dei Tribunali. Eat *pizza fritta* from a street vendor — fried pizza dough stuffed with ricotta and cicoli (pork cracklings). It's messy, heavy, and exactly right.

Visit an off-the-beaten alimentari (grocery) tucked into a side street — the kind with dusty bottles of local limoncello and wheels of aged pecorino behind the counter. This is where you buy things to take home.

**What to order:** Pizza fritta; local cheeses and provisions  
**Where:** Via dei Tribunali area; hidden alimentari  
**Cost:** €8–15

### Evening

Your final meal should be pizza — but the other pizza, the one you haven't had yet. Seek out *pizza fritta* done properly at a sit-down restaurant, or try *pizza marinara* (tomato, garlic, oregano, no cheese — the original) at a serious pizzeria you missed on Day 1. This is the meal worth crossing town for.

Order a *margherita DOC* made with buffalo mozzarella instead of fior di latte. Notice the difference. Finish with *delizia al limone* — the lemon cream cake that rivals sfogliatella for Neapolitan dessert supremacy.

This is the meal you'll remember when someone asks about Naples.

**What to order:** Pizza marinara or margherita DOC, delizia al limone  
**Where:** The best pizzeria you can book, Centro Storico or Old Town  
**Cost:** €20–40

**Day 3 Total Food Spend:** Budget ~€35 | Mid ~€50 | Splurge ~€90

## Pro Tips for Food Travelers

- **Carry small bills.** Many pizzerias, bakeries, and street vendors don't have change for €50 notes. Hit an ATM in the morning and break bills at larger establishments first.
- **Lunch is the value meal.** Many restaurants offer better prices at midday, and the food is identical. Save your splurge budget for one exceptional dinner, not three.
- **Learn two phrases:** "Posso avere..." (may I have) and "Il conto, per favore" (the check, please). Basic courtesy in Italian unlocks better service.
- **Don't photograph everything.** In family-run spots, eating beats documenting. They'll appreciate that you came to taste, not to perform.
