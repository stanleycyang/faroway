# Fiji Foodie Itinerary: 3 Days of Eating Your Way Through Fiji

Three days in Fiji taught me that the best meals here don't come from menus — they come from earth ovens dug into the ground, from women selling kokoda out of coolers at roadside stands, and from Indo-Fijian curry houses where the roti arrives wrapped in newspaper. This is an itinerary for eating with intention: every meal mapped, every dollar accounted for, every neighborhood walked so you don't have to guess.

## Before You Eat: Quick Notes

Fijians eat lunch between noon and 1pm, dinner around 7pm — arrive late and kitchens close. Tipping isn't expected but appreciated at resorts (10% is generous). Cash is essential beyond Nadi and Denarau; outer islands have no ATMs. For lovo feasts and resort dining, book 24 hours ahead. Remove your hat when entering villages, and when offered kava, drink the whole bowl in one gulp — it's respect, not a sipping drink.

## Day 1: Nadi — Gateway Flavors and Market Hunting

**Morning: The Curry Roti Start**

Skip the resort breakfast buffet. Drive into Nadi town proper and find a table at one of the Indo-Fijian curry houses clustered near the main market — Bulaccino Café is reliable, but the nameless spots with plastic chairs out front often serve better roti. Order a breakfast curry — lamb or goat, simmered since dawn — with two pieces of fresh paratha-style roti torn straight from the griddle. The bread should be flaky, slightly oily, perfect for scooping thick curry sauce. Wash it down with masala chai made with fresh ginger.

This is Fiji's dual heritage on a plate: 38% of the population traces roots to Indian laborers brought during British colonial rule, and their food has become as Fijian as kokoda. You'll pay FJ$12-18 ($5-8 USD) for a breakfast that could carry you to dinner.

**Estimated cost**: $5–$10

**Afternoon: Nadi Produce Market and Street Snacks**

Walk the Nadi Produce Market around 11am when vendors are fully stocked but the midday heat hasn't wilted everything. This isn't a tourist market — it's where locals buy cassava root by the armful, where bundles of rourou (taro leaves) sit in neat piles, where women sell homemade cassava cake wrapped in banana leaves for FJ$3-5 per piece.

Buy a piece of that cassava cake immediately. It's dense, sweet, coconut-rich, and completely different from anything Western dessert training prepares you for. Then find the fruit vendors: a whole papaya costs FJ$2-3, a hand of bananas FJ$1. Eat the papaya with lime squeezed over it, standing right there.

For a proper lunch, look for the kokoda sellers — sometimes inside the market, sometimes in coolers outside. This is Fiji's ceviche: raw walu (Spanish mackerel) or mahi-mahi marinated in fresh lime juice until "cooked," then folded into thick coconut cream with diced tomato, onion, and chili. Eaten from a plastic container with a fork, it's the single dish that defines Fijian flavor. Pay FJ$15-25 ($7-12 USD) for a portion that would cost triple at a resort.

**Estimated cost**: $10–$20

**Evening: Resort-Level Dining at Denarau**

Drive 20 minutes to Denarau Island for dinner — the marina complex has the highest concentration of proper restaurants. Head to Nadina Authentic Fijian Restaurant or one of the waterfront spots at Port Denarau for a sit-down meal that showcases Fijian ingredients with more polish.

Order the rourou as a side (taro leaves slow-cooked in coconut milk until silky and spinach-like), grilled reef fish with lolo (coconut cream) sauce, and finish with a proper vakalolo — a steamed pudding of cassava and banana in coconut caramel. A full dinner with one drink runs FJ$80-150 ($35-65 USD) depending on how you order.

The sunset over the marina is free, and spectacular.

**Estimated cost**: $35–$80

**Day 1 Total Food Spend**: Budget ~$50 | Mid ~$90 | Splurge ~$150

## Day 2: Coral Coast — Village Cooking and the Earth Oven

**Morning: Coastal Breakfast and Fruit Stands**

Drive an hour southeast along the Queens Highway to the Coral Coast — this resort-lined stretch has the island's best combination of local villages and accessible cooking experiences. Stop at any roadside fruit stand along the way; the pineapples grown on Viti Levu's northern slopes are sweeter than anything shipped to resorts.

For breakfast, find a spot at one of the small local cafés near Sigatoka town. Order scrambled eggs with rourou (the taro leaves make an appearance at every meal here) and thick toast with local butter. This isn't flashy food — it's Fijian home cooking served for FJ$15-20.

**Estimated cost**: $8–$15

**Afternoon: Lovo Feast and Village Visit**

This is the meal you came for. Book ahead with a village that offers lovo experiences — several along the Coral Coast welcome visitors for cultural tours that include the earth-oven feast. The process starts hours before you arrive: a pit is dug, filled with river stones, and heated with fire until the rocks glow. Whole pigs, chickens, fish wrapped in banana leaves, cassava, taro, and breadfruit are layered inside, covered with more leaves and earth, and left to steam-roast for 3-4 hours.

You'll arrive, participate in a kava ceremony (drink it all, it tastes like muddy water, you'll survive), watch a meke performance of traditional dancing, and then eat. The lovo meats have a smokiness that no oven replicates — the pork falls apart, the fish stays impossibly moist, the root vegetables caramelize in their own sugars.

This experience typically costs FJ$120-200 ($55-90 USD) per person all-inclusive, and it's worth every dollar. You won't eat again today. You won't want to.

**Estimated cost**: $55–$100

**Evening: Light Grazing Only**

After a proper lovo, dinner is unnecessary. If you must eat, walk to whatever small shop is nearest your accommodation and buy fresh coconut water (the vendor will hack it open with a machete) and perhaps a few pieces of fried cassava. This is a FJ$5-10 situation at most.

Or drive to Natadola Beach — considered Fiji's best mainland beach — and watch the sunset with nothing but fruit and the memory of that pork still on your tongue.

**Estimated cost**: $5–$15

**Day 2 Total Food Spend**: Budget ~$70 | Mid ~$110 | Splurge ~$180

## Day 3: Island Excursion — Eating on the Water

**Morning: Market Breakfast and Boat Prep**

Return to Nadi early and hit the market one more time before catching a boat to the Mamanuca Islands. Grab a cassava cake and fresh tropical fruit for the ride — mangoes if they're in season, papaya if not. A strong coffee from Bulaccino sets the caffeine baseline.

Pack cash: outer islands don't take cards, and you'll want to tip the boat crew and buy drinks.

**Estimated cost**: $5–$10

**Afternoon: Cloud 9 and Floating Lunch**

Take the 45-minute boat transfer to Cloud 9 — a floating platform bar anchored in the middle of the ocean with nothing but turquoise water in every direction. Yes, it's a tourist attraction. Yes, you should still go.

The pizza here is surprisingly legitimate — wood-fired, thin-crust, using imported Italian flour because someone involved actually cares. Order the seafood version with local prawns, or keep it simple with margherita. A pizza and two cocktails runs about FJ$80-100 ($35-45 USD), which sounds expensive until you remember you're eating on a platform in the middle of the Pacific Ocean while snorkeling between bites.

Swim, eat, drink, repeat. The boat back to Denarau departs around 3pm.

**Estimated cost**: $35–$60

**Evening: The Farewell Feast**

Your last dinner should be the best kokoda you can find, and that means either returning to Nadina in Denarau or booking at one of the resort restaurants that takes the dish seriously. Ask specifically if they make it fresh that day — kokoda that's been sitting loses its brightness.

Order kokoda as a starter, grilled mahi-mahi or walu as your main, rourou on the side, and finish with a passionfruit-based dessert if available. Drink a Fiji Bitter (the local lager) or a rum cocktail made with local sugarcane spirits.

This meal runs FJ$150-250 ($65-110 USD) for the full experience with drinks. Worth it. This is how Fiji tastes when everything aligns.

**Estimated cost**: $65–$120

**Day 3 Total Food Spend**: Budget ~$105 | Mid ~$150 | Splurge ~$250

## Pro Tips for Food Travelers

- **Kokoda freshness matters**: Always ask when it was made. The best versions are prepared within hours of serving; anything from yesterday has lost its citrus punch and turned mushy.

- **Kava is not optional**: When visiting villages, the kava ceremony is a cultural gateway. Drink the full bowl, clap once before, clap three times after, and say "bula" with feeling. The taste is terrible. The respect it earns you is invaluable.

- **Bring ziplock bags to the market**: Vendors sell things loose or in thin plastic bags that leak. If you're buying kokoda or cut fruit to transport, your own containers save your day bag.

- **Tip with intention, not obligation**: Fijians don't expect tips at local spots, but leaving FJ$5-10 at places where someone genuinely took care of you means more than 20% at a resort where it's automatic. Read the room.
