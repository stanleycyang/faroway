# Dubai Foodie Itinerary: 3 Days of Eating Your Way Through Dubai

Three days. One city built on ambition and spice routes. The mission: eat Dubai properly — from the shawarma stands where taxi drivers queue at 2 AM to the dining rooms where chefs plate gold leaf onto desserts that cost more than your flight. This is the itinerary I wish someone had handed me before I landed.

## Before You Eat: Quick Notes

Dubai eats late. Lunch rarely happens before 1 PM, and dinner reservations before 8 PM mark you as a tourist. Credit cards work everywhere except the old souks — bring cash for Deira. Tipping 10–15% is appreciated but not obligatory; service charges often appear on bills. During Ramadan, eating in public during daylight hours is illegal, but hotel restaurants serve discreetly. Book Friday brunch at least a week ahead — it's Dubai's secular religion.

## Day 1: Downtown Dubai — Towers, Fountains, and the New Money Feast

### Morning: Breakfast with a View

Start where Dubai starts for most visitors — Downtown Dubai, in the shadow of the Burj Khalifa. But skip the tourist traps in Dubai Mall and head to one of the neighborhood's Arabic breakfast spots where Emirati families gather on weekends. You want *shakshuka* bubbling in a cast-iron pan, scooped up with fresh khubz bread, alongside creamy labneh drizzled with olive oil and za'atar. Order a pot of karak chai — the spiced tea that powers this city — and watch the fountain basin fill with morning light.

**Estimated cost**: $15–$25 for a full Arabic breakfast spread.

### Afternoon: Dubai Mall Food Hall and Spice Exploration

Here's what nobody tells you about Dubai Mall: beyond the tourist-facing food court lies a genuinely excellent international food hall. Grab **hummus and falafel** from one of the Lebanese counters — the chickpea dip should be warm, loose, and drowning in good olive oil; the falafel crisp-shelled with a bright green herb interior. This is lunch for $12, eaten standing at a counter while shoppers stream past with bags from Cartier.

After, take the Metro to Deira and lose an hour in the Spice Souk. The vendors here aren't pushy — they know their product sells itself. Buy saffron at half the European price, dried limes for your kitchen back home, and baharat spice blends in brown paper bags. The air smells like cardamom and commerce.

**Estimated cost**: $20–$35 including spice purchases.

### Evening: Downtown Dinner with Fountain Views

Return to Downtown Dubai for dinner as the city cools and the fountain show begins. You have two paths here: the mid-range restaurant with terrace seating where you'll eat grilled lamb chops with Arabic rice while water dances to Whitney Houston, or the hotel fine-dining room where tasting menus run 12 courses and the sommelier knows your preferences by the third pour.

Order **Al Machboos**, the UAE's national dish — spiced rice layered with slow-cooked lamb, the meat falling apart at the suggestion of a fork. The rice should be fragrant with dried lime and cardamom, slightly sticky, deeply savory. This is Emirati food at its finest.

**Estimated cost**: $60–$180 depending on venue and drinks.

**Day 1 total food spend**: Budget ~$95 | Mid ~$140 | Splurge ~$240

## Day 2: Old Dubai — Creek-Side Heritage and the Immigrant Kitchen

### Morning: Deira and the South Asian Breakfast Trail

Cross the creek on an *abra* — those wooden boats cost 1 dirham and deliver you to Deira's chaotic heart in four minutes. This is Old Dubai, where the city's South Asian workforce eats extraordinarily well for very little money. Find a Pakistani or Indian cafeteria near the Gold Souk and order *paratha* with spiced chickpea curry, or *dosa* — those crispy rice-flour crepes — with coconut chutney. The room will be fluorescent-lit, the plastic tables sticky with yesterday's chai, the food transcendent.

**Estimated cost**: $5–$10 for a massive breakfast.

### Afternoon: Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood and the Hidden Courtyard Lunch

Walk south along the creek to Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood, where restored wind-tower houses now hold galleries and courtyard cafés. This is Dubai before oil, before towers, before ambition went vertical. Duck into a converted heritage house for lunch and order lamb ouzi — slow-roasted until it shreds, served over rice with toasted nuts and crispy onions.

After, find the XVA Art Hotel's courtyard café, tucked behind unmarked wooden doors. The menu is vegetarian, Middle Eastern, and perfect for the setting: fattoush salads, muhammara dip, mint lemonade in the shade of a 100-year-old building. This is one of Dubai's genuinely hidden spots, and the Wi-Fi password is written on a chalkboard next to the books.

**Estimated cost**: $25–$40 for lunch and coffee.

### Evening: The Shawarma Pilgrimage

Tonight, forget fine dining. Tonight, you hunt shawarma.

The great Dubai shawarma debate rages between several late-night spots, but the principles remain constant: the meat must rotate on vertical spits all day, basted in its own fat; the bread must be fresh from a drum oven; the garlic sauce should be aggressive, unapologetic, likely to follow you home. **Shawarma** in Dubai means chicken or lamb, wrapped in thin flatbread with pickled turnips, fries tucked inside (yes, inside), and enough garlic paste to ward off romance for a week.

Find a shawarma joint in Deira where the taxi drivers eat. Order at the counter. Pay $4. Stand outside on the pavement with your wrapped bundle of perfection and understand why this city runs on these things.

For something more substantial, add **kunafa** for dessert from a nearby sweets shop — that shredded pastry with molten cheese and orange-blossom syrup is best eaten hot, with the cheese still stretching.

**Estimated cost**: $15–$30 for shawarma dinner with dessert and drinks.

**Day 2 total food spend**: Budget ~$45 | Mid ~$80 | Splurge ~$80 (this day is supposed to be cheap)

## Day 3: The Marina, The Desert, and the Farewell Feast

### Morning: Dubai Marina Brunch Culture

Friday brunch is Dubai's great social equalizer — a three-hour marathon of eating and drinking that justifies the previous week's gym sessions. Book ahead at a Dubai Marina restaurant with waterfront views and prepare yourself: brunch here means stations. Arabic mezze. Sushi. Carvery. Dessert tables that stretch longer than airport runways.

Pace yourself. Start with cold dishes — Arabic salads, smoked salmon, hummus again (always hummus). Move to the hot stations. The **camel burger** often appears at upscale brunches, served slider-style — the meat is leaner than beef, slightly gamey, surprisingly delicate. End with something sweet, knowing you've eaten enough to skip lunch entirely.

**Estimated cost**: $80–$200+ including drinks packages.

### Afternoon: Alserkal Avenue and the Creative Snack

Sleep off brunch with a walk through Alserkal Avenue in Al Quoz, the warehouse arts district where Dubai's creative class actually hangs out. Between gallery visits, stop at one of the specialty coffee roasters — this neighborhood takes its flat whites seriously — and grab a light afternoon bite. The cafés here serve avocado toasts and grain bowls, but also excellent Arabic coffee with dates, the traditional Emirati welcome gesture.

This is where to buy edible souvenirs: specialty chocolates filled with camel milk, date syrups, small-batch hot sauces made by Dubai entrepreneurs.

**Estimated cost**: $15–$30 for coffee and snacks.

### Evening: The Desert or the Skyline — Your Final Meal

You have a choice. Path one: book a sunset desert safari with dinner under the stars. You'll dune-bash until your stomach tests its limits, watch the sun drop behind sand mountains, then eat grilled meats and Arabic mezze while someone performs traditional dances. **Luqaimat** — those sweet fried dumplings drizzled with date syrup — will appear for dessert, crispy on the outside, soft within, perfect with Arabic coffee. This is theatrical, touristy, and genuinely magical.

Path two: stay in the city. Book a table at a Dubai Marina rooftop with views of the yacht-lined waterfront, or cross to DIFC for upscale dining in the financial district. Order a final round of everything Dubai does best: grilled lamb, mezze spread across the table, good Lebanese wine, baklava to finish.

Either way, eat slowly. You're saying goodbye.

**Estimated cost**: $75–$250 depending on experience choice.

**Day 3 total food spend**: Budget ~$170 | Mid ~$280 | Splurge ~$480

## Pro Tips for Food Travelers

- **The best shawarma joints close when they sell out**, often by midnight. Go early if you're targeting a specific spot. If there's no line, be suspicious.
- **Water is expensive in restaurants** — AED 30–50 for a bottle isn't unusual at upscale spots. Ask for tap water; it's perfectly safe and rarely offered.
- **Friday brunch books out weeks ahead** at popular spots. Decide before you fly, book online, and bring your appetite.
- **Deira's food scene operates on South Asian timing**: breakfast cafés open by 6 AM for shift workers, and the best plates come out before 8. Don't sleep in if you want the good stuff.
