Three days in Melbourne taught me that this city doesn't just have good food — it has *opinions* about food. Strong ones. The kind of opinions that result in baristas who've trained for years, Vietnamese restaurants where three generations perfect a single broth, and Greek communities that argue passionately about which lamb is cooked correctly. This itinerary is what I wish someone had handed me before I arrived hungry.

## Before You Eat: Quick Notes

Melbourne runs late. Brunch stretches to 2pm, dinner reservations before 7pm feel oddly empty, and the best laneway bars don't hit their stride until 10pm. Tipping isn't expected, but 10 percent for excellent service is appreciated. Card works everywhere — I didn't touch cash once. Book dinner at popular spots 3–5 days ahead, especially weekends. And please, do not order drip coffee at a specialty cafe. They will serve you, but they will judge you.

## Day 1: CBD Laneways — Coffee, Markets, and Hidden Bars

**Morning: The Flat White That Started Everything**

Start where Melbourne's coffee reputation was built: the narrow lanes off Flinders Street. Degraves Street is the obvious choice, and it's obvious for a reason — the independent coffee shops here have been perfecting the flat white since before the rest of the world knew what it was. Find a seat at one of the cramped sidewalk tables, order a flat white with your choice of single-origin beans, and pair it with avocado toast. Yes, it's the brunch dish Melbourne made globally famous, and yes, it's still done better here than wherever you came from. Expect thick sourdough, properly seasoned avocado with good olive oil, maybe some feta or dukkah if you're lucky. The whole thing costs around $22–$28 and you'll understand why Melbourne guards its breakfast reputation so fiercely.

*Estimated cost: $25–$35*

**Afternoon: Queen Victoria Market and Chinatown**

Queen Victoria Market is best experienced on a Saturday, but any day rewards a hungry walker. This isn't a tourist market with overpriced trinkets — it's where Melbourne actually shops. Head to the deli hall first: sample olives, pick up a borek from the Turkish stall, grab some marinated artichokes. For a proper lunch, find the meat pie stands. Get one with tomato sauce — the proper Australian combination that's been fueling this country since forever. A good pie runs $7–$9 and should be flaky, meaty, and absolutely not fancy.

Walk off the pie heading southeast to Little Bourke Street, where Melbourne's Chinatown has operated since the 1850s gold rush. By 2pm, you're ready for dim sum. Yum cha here is serious business — bamboo steamers of har gow, siu mai, and char siu bao circulating on carts, the clatter of chopsticks, families spanning four generations at round tables. Budget around $35–$50 per person depending on how many dumplings you chase down.

*Estimated cost: $45–$65*

**Evening: Laneway Dining and Drinking**

The CBD laneways hide some of Melbourne's most celebrated restaurants behind graffitied walls and unmarked doors. For dinner, you want somewhere that captures the energy of the city — the kind of place where the room is loud, the menu is Southeast Asian–influenced, and the food arrives fast and punchy. Think coconut curries, crispy pork, kingfish sashimi with chili. Melbourne does this style exceptionally well. Budget $60–$80 for dinner without drinks at a mid-range option; splurge diners should expect $120–$150 with wine.

After dinner, don't go home. The laneway bar scene is Melbourne's highlight after dark. Find a speakeasy behind an unmarked door, order a cocktail made by someone who takes it very seriously, and understand why people fly here just to drink in alleys.

*Estimated cost: $75–$150*

**Day 1 Total Food Spend**: Budget ~$145 | Mid ~$200 | Splurge ~$350

## Day 2: Richmond and Collingwood — Pho, Craft Beer, and the Creative Edge

**Morning: Slow Start in Collingwood**

Collingwood wakes up late and unapologetically. Find a corner cafe on Smith Street where the barista has a beard you could lose a small bird in, and the sourdough is baked in-house. This is Melbourne's creative heart — the clientele is tattoo artists, designers, and people who've stayed out too late at the breweries nearby. Keep breakfast simple: poached eggs, good bread, excellent coffee. The point is to prepare your stomach for what's coming.

*Estimated cost: $20–$30*

**Afternoon: Victoria Street and the Pho Belt**

Walk or tram to Victoria Street in Richmond, where Melbourne's Vietnamese community has built a strip so authentic that homesick expats make pilgrimages here. This is where you eat pho — not the watered-down version other cities serve, but a proper bowl with broth that's been simmering for 12 hours, thin slices of beef that cook in the heat, and a plate of Thai basil, bean sprouts, and chili that you doctor to taste. A large bowl runs $16–$19 and genuinely feeds you for hours.

Don't stop at pho. Walk the street and hit the banh mi shops for a $8 baguette that rivals anything in Saigon. Get a Vietnamese iced coffee from one of the older cafes where the owners still drip it slow through metal filters. Pick up a bag of fresh rice paper rolls if you're somehow still hungry.

*Estimated cost: $30–$45*

**Evening: Craft Beer and Pub Dinner**

Collingwood and Richmond are ground zero for Melbourne's craft beer obsession. The breweries here aren't precious about it — they're industrial spaces with concrete floors, long communal tables, and very good beer. Some have food programs that go far beyond bar snacks: wood-fired pizzas, whole rotisserie chickens, burgers stacked properly. Pick a brewery, order a tasting paddle, and settle in for the kind of relaxed dinner where you end up talking to strangers at the next table. Budget $50–$70 for beers and food; splurge on a brewery dinner with paired beers for $100–$140.

*Estimated cost: $65–$140*

**Day 2 Total Food Spend**: Budget ~$115 | Mid ~$180 | Splurge ~$315

## Day 3: Fitzroy, South Yarra, and the Farewell Feast

**Morning: Brunch on Brunswick Street**

Fitzroy owns Melbourne's bohemian soul, and Brunswick Street is its main artery. The brunch here is competitive — cafes have been one-upping each other for decades. Find somewhere with a queue (always a good sign) and order generously: shakshuka with house-baked bread, or a full breakfast with proper bacon and eggs cooked exactly how you ask. The coffee will be excellent because anything less would be embarrassing. Budget $30–$40 and don't rush.

*Estimated cost: $30–$45*

**Afternoon: Abbotsford Convent and Chapel Street Grazing**

Take the short walk to Abbotsford Convent, a former convent turned arts precinct with gardens, galleries, and a handful of cafes that feel wonderfully separate from the city. This is where you have a long, slow coffee, maybe a slice of cake, and let the morning settle.

Then, recalibrate. Head south to Chapel Street, which runs from South Yarra through Prahran and captures Melbourne's upscale dining scene in miniature. This isn't about one big meal — it's about grazing. A dozen oysters at a wine bar ($30–$40). Some charcuterie and a glass of something local ($25–$35). Maybe a serve of Greek souvlaki from one of the gyro shops, because Melbourne has the largest Greek population outside Athens and you shouldn't leave without tasting lamb wrapped in soft pita with tzatziki dripping down your wrist ($15–$20).

*Estimated cost: $55–$90*

**Evening: The Meal Worth Ending On**

Your last dinner in Melbourne should be memorable, and Melbourne delivers. South Yarra and Prahran have restaurants worth booking days ahead — the kind where the tasting menu tells a story, the wine list is deep, and the service makes you feel like you've been coming for years. You're looking at $150–$250 per person for a splurge dinner, and it's worth every dollar. Ask for a wine pairing; this is Australian wine country adjacent, and sommeliers here know their regions intimately.

Budget travelers can end just as happily: find a neighborhood Italian with handmade pasta for $25–$35, or a modern Australian bistro where the steak is cooked over embers and costs less than you'd expect ($40–$60). The point is to end well.

*Estimated cost: $80–$250*

**Day 3 Total Food Spend**: Budget ~$165 | Mid ~$240 | Splurge ~$385

## Pro Tips for Food Travelers

- **Weekday lunch is your secret weapon.** Many high-end restaurants offer lunch menus at half the dinner price. That tasting menu you can't afford at 8pm? Check if they serve at noon.

- **The best coffee shops don't have signs.** If it looks like a garage with a coffee machine, it probably is — and the flat white will be perfect. Ask locals, not Google.

- **Victoria Street pho is best before 2pm.** The broth at most places runs continuously, but the morning batches are richer. By dinner, it's still good, but different.

- **Melbourne's weather earns its reputation.** I watched sunshine turn to sideways rain turn back to sunshine in twenty minutes. Carry a compact umbrella and don't trust the morning sky.
