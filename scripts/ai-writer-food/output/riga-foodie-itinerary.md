Three days in Riga taught me that Latvian food is what happens when harsh winters meet a people who refuse to eat poorly. This is a cuisine built on rye bread so dark it's almost black, fish smoked until it tastes like the forest, and pork in every form the animal allows. My mission was simple: eat everything, note everything, and return with the itinerary I wished someone had handed me on arrival.

## Before You Eat: Quick Notes

Latvians eat dinner early — by 7pm, most locals have already ordered. Restaurants take cards everywhere, but market stalls often prefer cash in small bills. Tipping is 10% at sit-down restaurants; at markets and cafés, just round up. Book weekend dinners at popular spots a day ahead, especially in the Old Town. Latvians are reserved but warm once you show genuine interest in their food — ask questions about ingredients and watch faces light up.

## Day 1: The Old Town and the Zeppelin Market

**Morning — Old Town, 8:30am**

Start where Riga started: the UNESCO-listed medieval center. Find a table at one of the cafés near Riga Cathedral and order what every Latvian eats for breakfast — dark rye bread (rupjmaize) with fresh butter, soft cheese, and cold cuts. This bread is the foundation of everything here: dense, slightly sour, chewy with caraway seeds. It's not a side dish. It's the main character.

Pair it with a pot of strong Baltic coffee. The morning light hits the cathedral stones, the cobbles are still quiet, and you're eating the same breakfast Latvians have eaten for centuries.

*Budget: €6–8 · Mid: €10–12 · Splurge: €15–18 (at a historic hotel café)*

**Afternoon — Central Market, 11:30am**

Walk fifteen minutes south to the Riga Central Market, housed in five massive WWI zeppelin hangars that someone decided to fill with food instead of bombs. This is the largest market in Europe, and it's not a tourist attraction pretending to be a market — it's where Rigans actually shop.

Go directly to the fish pavilion. The smoked fish counters will stop you in your tracks: golden-brown herring, oily sprats bundled in newspaper, whole smoked eel glistening under the lights. Point at what looks good and the vendor will slice it for you. A few euros gets you enough smoked fish to make a grown person weep with joy. Eat it standing, with more of that rye bread and a cold Latvian beer from the market bar.

Wander the other pavilions — the meat hall with its hanging sausages, the dairy section with wheels of caraway cheese, the pickle vendors with barrels of fermented everything. Buy a couple of piragi (small baked buns stuffed with smoked bacon and onion) from any bakery stall. They cost almost nothing and taste like someone turned a perfect BLT into a pastry.

*Lunch spend — Budget: €8–12 · Mid: €15–20 · Splurge: €25–30 (with extras)*

**Evening — Centrs, 7pm**

For dinner, head to the district between the Old Town and the Art Nouveau buildings — Centrs, where Rigans go when they want to eat well without tourist markup. This is where to try pelēkie zirņi ar speķi: grey peas with smoked bacon. It's traditionally a New Year's dish, but good restaurants serve it year-round. The peas are dense and earthy, the bacon salty and crisp, the whole thing humble in a way that makes you trust it immediately.

Order a glass of Riga Black Balsam on the side — the herbal liqueur that's been made here since 1752. It tastes like pine forests and old medicine and bitter chocolate all at once. Mix it with blackcurrant juice if you're not ready to take it straight.

*Dinner spend — Budget: €15–20 · Mid: €25–35 · Splurge: €50–70*

**Day 1 Total**: Budget ~€35 · Mid ~€55 · Splurge ~€115

## Day 2: The Left Bank and What the Tourists Miss

**Morning — Āgenskalns, 9am**

Cross the Daugava River to Āgenskalns, the hip left-bank neighborhood that most visitors never find. On Saturday mornings, the Kalnciema Quarter Market fills a wooden courtyard with organic farmers, bakers, and elderly women selling jars of things they pickled last autumn. This is where Riga's creative class buys breakfast.

Find the stall selling freshly fried potato pancakes (kartupeļu pankūkas) — crispy-edged, dense in the middle, served with sour cream so thick the spoon stands up. Eat them at the communal tables surrounded by wooden houses and locals in expensive eyewear. A small coffee from the specialty roaster, a couple of pancakes: this is around €7 and will hold you until well past noon.

*Budget: €5–7 · Mid: €8–12 · Splurge: €15–18 (with pastries and extras)*

**Afternoon — Āgenskalns Market, 1pm**

Stay on the left bank and walk to Āgenskalns Market — the neighborhood's covered market that operates completely off the tourist trail. This is where to find the best cold cuts in Riga: thick-cut smoked bacon, headcheese, blood sausage sliced to order.

Build yourself a lunch from the stalls. A chunk of smoked pork knuckle. More of that rye bread. A jar of pickled garlic or fermented cabbage. A pastry from the bakery counter. Find a bench in the adjacent park and spread it all out. This is how Latvians eat when they're not performing for visitors: simply, well, with excellent ingredients and zero ceremony.

*Lunch spend — Budget: €7–10 · Mid: €12–18 · Splurge: €20–25*

**Evening — Pārdaugava, 7:30pm**

Stay on this side of the river for dinner in Pārdaugava, the broader left-bank district that feels like a different city — more local, more residential, more real. This is where to try modern Latvian cuisine: chefs who grew up on grey peas and smoked fish but studied in Copenhagen or Berlin.

Look for restaurants serving updated versions of traditional dishes — pike-perch from the Daugava with foraged greens, wild mushroom soup thickened with rye flour, pork belly braised until it gives up completely. This is the meal to splurge on if you're going to splurge. The cooking is serious but the prices (compared to similar quality in Western Europe) are laughably reasonable.

Order a bottle of Latvian wine — yes, it exists, from the country's few vineyards — or stick with local craft beer.

*Dinner spend — Budget: €18–25 · Mid: €35–50 · Splurge: €70–100*

**Day 2 Total**: Budget ~€35 · Mid ~€60 · Splurge ~€140

## Day 3: Art Nouveau and the Farewell Feast

**Morning — Art Nouveau District (Quiet Centre), 9:30am**

Your final day begins in the Art Nouveau District, also called the Quiet Centre — 800 buildings with the most elaborate facades in the world. But before you start photographing the stonework on Alberta iela, find a café in one of these beautiful buildings and order a proper Latvian breakfast spread: cold smoked fish, sliced cucumber and tomato, rye bread, cheese, and a soft-boiled egg. Coffee from beans roasted in Riga.

This breakfast feels different in a building from 1903, with geometric ironwork outside the window and jugendstil details on the ceiling above. It's around €12 and worth every cent.

*Budget: €8–10 · Mid: €12–15 · Splurge: €18–25*

**Afternoon — Centrs and Old Town, 1pm**

Use your afternoon for the eating you haven't done yet. Walk back through Centrs toward the Old Town, stopping at the specialty food shops that line these streets. Buy honey from Latvian bees. Sample fermented rye drinks. Find the bakeries selling speķa rauši — another form of bacon bun, this one shaped like a crescent moon.

For a proper lunch, find a casual spot in the Old Town serving traditional Latvian soups — sorrel soup (skābeņu zupa), cold beet soup (aukstā zupa) if it's summer, or a thick meat soup with barley and root vegetables. Order more piragi on the side. Have a half-liter of local beer. The bill will be around €12 for more food than any reasonable person needs.

*Lunch spend — Budget: €8–12 · Mid: €15–22 · Splurge: €28–35*

**Evening — Old Town, 7:30pm**

Your final meal should be the one worth remembering. Return to the Old Town for a dinner that pulls together everything Latvian cuisine does well. Start with smoked eel — the best version you've had all trip. Follow with pork knuckle, slow-roasted until the skin shatters and the meat falls apart. Or pike-perch if you want to end with the river. 

Finish with rye bread ice cream or a dessert made from birch sap. Have a final glass of Black Balsam, this time taken straight. You've earned it.

If the night is warm and you're not ready to stop, walk to Folkklubs Ala Pagrabs — a medieval cellar with Latvian craft beer and folk music that goes late. It's the kind of place that doesn't exist anywhere else.

*Dinner spend — Budget: €20–30 · Mid: €40–55 · Splurge: €80–120*

**Day 3 Total**: Budget ~€40 · Mid ~€75 · Splurge ~€180

## Pro Tips for Food Travelers

- **Go to Central Market before 10am.** By noon, the fish pavilion is crowded and the best stuff is picked over. Early morning is when the serious shoppers come.

- **Ask for recommendations in Latvian.** Even just "Kas ir labs?" (What's good?) with a smile will change how vendors treat you. They'll slice you the good stuff from the back.

- **Don't skip the left bank.** Āgenskalns and Kalnciema Quarter have better food at better prices than the Old Town, plus you'll actually be around Latvians instead of other tourists.

- **Riga Black Balsam comes in three versions.** Original (45% ABV, intensely bitter), blackcurrant (30%, sweeter), and cream (17%, for people who want dessert). Try all three, decide later.
