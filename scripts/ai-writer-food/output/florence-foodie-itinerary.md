# Florence Foodie Itinerary: 3 Days of Eating Your Way Through Florence

Three days. One city. An unreasonable number of meals. Florence rewards the obsessive eater — this is a place where a tripe sandwich from a cart can rival a €100 bistecca, where gelato is a twice-daily commitment, and where every neighborhood guards its own version of what "real Florentine food" means. I walked 47 kilometers across these three days, and I regret nothing except the pants I brought.

## Before You Eat: Quick Notes

Florentines eat late by northern standards — lunch starts at 12:30pm, dinner rarely before 8pm. Most trattorias are cash-preferred; cards work but often with a minimum. Tipping isn't expected but rounding up a few euros for good service is appreciated. Book any sit-down dinner in advance, especially for bistecca — serious steak houses sell out their best cuts by early evening. And dress modestly if you're combining food walks with church visits; bare shoulders mean no entry, and the fines for eating on church steps are real.

## Day 1: San Lorenzo and the Market Heart

**Morning — Mercato Centrale, 8:30am**

Start where Florence starts its day: the ground floor of Mercato Centrale in San Lorenzo. Skip the renovated upstairs food hall (that's for later) and head to the original market level, where butchers and produce vendors have worked the same stalls for generations. Find a bar counter inside and order a *caffè* standing up — it costs half what you'd pay sitting — alongside a freshly baked *schiacciata*, the Florentine flatbread that's crispier and oilier than focaccia, dimpled with olive oil and scattered with coarse salt. The plain version costs €2-3; stuffed with prosciutto and stracchino cheese, maybe €5. Eat it warm. Walk the aisles. Watch elderly Florentines argue with butchers over the cut of their tripe.

**Cost: €7-12**

**Afternoon — Lampredotto Cart Crawl, 12:30pm**

This is your initiation. *Lampredotto* — the fourth stomach of a cow, slow-simmered and stuffed into a crusty roll — is Florence's definitive street food, and you need to eat it from a cart, not a restaurant. The best carts cluster around Mercato Centrale and Sant'Ambrogio. Order yours *bagnato* (the roll dipped in the cooking broth) with *salsa verde* and a little spicy oil. The texture is tender, almost silky; the taste is deeply savory without being funky. A sandwich runs €4-5, and it's one of the best €5 you'll spend anywhere in Italy.

After your sandwich, walk through the San Lorenzo leather market — dodging the aggressive sellers — and loop back through the Mercato Centrale's upper floor for a late-afternoon *aperitivo*. The upstairs food hall has decent options for a glass of Chianti (€6-8) and some sliced *finocchiona* (fennel salami, €5-7). Not the most authentic setting, but the products are real and you can rest your feet.

**Cost: €15-25**

**Evening — Trattoria in Centro Storico, 8pm**

Night one calls for the dish that put Florence on the carnivore's map: *bistecca alla Fiorentina*. This is a massive T-bone from Chianina cattle, grilled rare over charcoal, served by the kilo. You're not ordering this alone — it's meant for two or three people, priced at €45-60 per kilo (expect 1.2-1.5kg minimum). The outside should be charred, the inside cool and red. Ask for *olio nuovo* to drizzle on top if it's autumn.

Book a table away from the Duomo — Centro Storico has excellent options on the side streets near Piazza della Signoria, where the same quality costs 30% less than the tourist frontline. Add a *contorno* of white beans with sage, a carafe of house Chianti, and you're looking at €50-80 per person for a proper feast. Don't order pasta first; the steak is the meal.

**Cost: €50-90**

**Day 1 Total Food Spend:** Budget ~€75 | Mid ~€120 | Splurge ~€200

## Day 2: Oltrarno — Artisan Quarter, Artisan Food

**Morning — Café Culture South of the Arno, 9am**

Cross the Ponte Vecchio early, before the jewelry-gawkers clog the bridge, and find a neighborhood café in Oltrarno. This is the artisan quarter — bookbinders, goldsmiths, furniture restorers — and the food leans traditional. Order *caffè* and a *cornetto* (€3-4) and sit outside if you can. The pace is slower here; locals read newspapers on paper.

After coffee, walk toward Piazza Santo Spirito and stop at any *forno* (bakery) for a second *schiacciata* — this time *all'uva* if they have it, the sweet grape-studded version that's seasonal to autumn. Even out of season, the savory variations here tend to be better than the tourist-market versions across the river.

**Cost: €8-12**

**Afternoon — Long Lunch at a Real Trattoria, 1pm**

Oltrarno is where Florentines eat when they're not performing for tourists. Find a *trattoria* on the side streets near Santo Spirito — tables covered in paper, handwritten menus, no English necessary because the food speaks clearly enough. Order *ribollita*, the thick bread-and-vegetable soup that's more stew than broth, made from yesterday's bread and whatever vegetables were cheap at market. It costs €8-12 and feeds you for hours. Follow with *pappardelle al cinghiale* — wide ribbons of fresh pasta with wild boar ragù, earthy and rich. That's another €12-15.

This is a two-course, one-wine lunch. Don't rush it. The trattorie in Oltrarno expect you to stay, to talk, to order a *caffè* at the end. Budget €25-40 per person with wine.

After lunch, walk off the pasta through the Oltrarno artisan workshops — most welcome visitors, and watching a bookbinder at work pairs well with a food coma.

**Cost: €25-45**

**Evening — Aperitivo to Dinner, Santo Spirito, 7pm**

Return to Piazza Santo Spirito for the evening *passeggiata* and aperitivo. This piazza is the heart of Florentine nightlife without feeling like a club district — outdoor bars, Negronis (Florence invented them, after all), and locals who've been meeting at these same tables for decades. A Negroni or Aperol Spritz runs €8-12 and usually comes with snacks.

For dinner, stay in Oltrarno but seek out the restaurants that locals protect. Tonight is for what you didn't eat yesterday: maybe *peposo* (peppery beef stew), or *trippa alla Fiorentina* if the lampredotto convinced you. Keep it two courses, keep it neighborhood. €35-50 per person with wine.

**Cost: €50-70**

**Day 2 Total Food Spend:** Budget ~€85 | Mid ~€130 | Splurge ~€220

## Day 3: Santa Croce and the Farewell Feast

**Morning — Espresso and Pastry Near the Basilica, 8:30am**

Your last morning belongs to Santa Croce, the basilica quarter with some of Florence's best coffee bars. Stand at the counter like a local, order a *caffè macchiato* and a *bombolone* (cream-filled doughnut, €2-3), and watch the neighborhood wake up. The gelaterias here are among the city's best — note them for later.

**Cost: €5-8**

**Afternoon — Sant'Ambrogio Market and Grazing Lunch, 12pm**

Skip the formal lunch. Instead, head to Mercato di Sant'Ambrogio, the market where Florentines actually shop. It's rougher than Mercato Centrale, less photogenic, better. Buy *porchetta* sliced from the whole pig (€5-6 for a generous portion), eat it with bread at the stand-up counters. Try *coccoli* — fried dough balls served with prosciutto and stracchino — from a friggitoria nearby. Get a few slices of aged pecorino. This is grazing as sport.

After the market, walk to Santa Croce's gelateria strip. Now is the time for a double-scoop cone: *crema* (egg custard) and *pistachio*, or *fior di latte* with *nocciola*. Florence invented artisanal gelato; honor the birthplace. A proper cone: €3-5.

**Cost: €20-30**

**Evening — The Meal You'll Remember, 8:30pm**

Your last dinner should be the one worth traveling for. Return to Centro Storico or venture to San Niccolò, the quiet neighborhood below Piazzale Michelangelo, for a restaurant that takes its craft seriously. This is the night for a proper *degustazione* if you want it — or simply the best version of what you've already fallen for. Maybe the *bistecca* again, because you understand it now. Maybe a dish you never expected: roasted pigeon, rabbit cacciatore, pasta with fresh porcini.

This meal earns the splurge: €70-120 per person, wine included, at a place that respects the ingredients the way Florence always has. Walk home along the Arno, past the illuminated Ponte Vecchio, and let the city keep you one more hour.

**Cost: €70-130**

**Day 3 Total Food Spend:** Budget ~€100 | Mid ~€170 | Splurge ~€280

## Pro Tips for Food Travelers

- **Order water from public fountains**: The *fontanelle* are everywhere, the water is excellent, and you'll save €2-3 per restaurant meal by not buying bottles.
- **Lunch is your value meal**: The same *trattoria* charging €18 for dinner pasta often has a €10 lunch menu. Eat heavily midday, graze at night.
- **Cash speeds everything up**: Cards technically work, but smaller trattorias and all market stalls prefer cash. The lampredotto cart doesn't have a card reader, and you want that sandwich.
- **Avoid the Duomo-adjacent restaurants entirely**: The quality drops and prices double within 100 meters of the cathedral. Walk five minutes in any direction and you're eating with Florentines again.
